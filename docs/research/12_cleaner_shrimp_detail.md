# Cleaner shrimp behavior: evidence for ReefLive

Research update: 2026-10-12. Companion to [the invertebrate research update](11_invertebrate_behavior_detail.md). Source links were checked against species and study identity. Findings below are research notes, not implemented behavior.

**Scope.** “Observed” describes measured results. “Inference” suggests simulation behavior unmeasured in this tank. These species should not share one generic cleaner routine.

## Skunk cleaner shrimp — *Lysmata amboinensis*

**Observed cleaning and signalling.** Cameras at two Red Sea cleaning stations recorded 241 shrimp–fish interactions across 14 client species. Predatory clients were cleaned in a smaller fraction of visits than non-predatory clients. Shrimp rocked their white front legs more often when a predatory client approached; antennae tapping preceded physical cleaning. In laboratory trials, dark visual stimuli and reduced illumination also elicited leg rocking. Rocking is a *possible* signal of cleaner identity or intent, but a broad light cue might drive it. The study’s 31.4 hours at two stations do not establish a universal visit rate. [Caves, Chen & Johnsen (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6769148/).

**Observed parasite removal.** In controlled grouper challenges, *L. amboinensis* reduced a monogenean parasite on fish during both day and night trials. Effectiveness varied by shrimp, parasite stage, and time of day. A separate culture experiment found that this shrimp consumed off-host parasite eggs and larvae and reduced subsequent infection. These results support cleaning and environmental foraging, but do not prove that one shrimp will clear an outbreak in a mixed aquarium. [Vaughan et al. (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-32293-6); [Militz & Hutson (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4338302/).

**Observed molting and reproduction.** In Fiedler’s small captive pairing experiment, female-phase shrimp paired with another female-phase individual produced fertile clutches, while separated individuals produced only infertile eggs. Paired animals staggered molts and alternated reproductive roles; anatomy showed functional male and female tissue in the female phase. This supports functional simultaneous hermaphroditism and the need for a mate for fertile clutches. It does not mean that an isolated shrimp cannot carry eggs or that larvae will survive in a display tank. [Fiedler (1998), author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/29737105_Functional_Simultaneous_Hermaphroditism_in_Female-Phase_Lysmata_amboinensis_Decapoda_Caridea_Hippolytidae).

**Inference for the simulation.** Separate advertisement, antenna contact, and physical cleaning. Track molt and egg carrying independently. These are proposed behaviors, not measured probabilities for Tee's tank. A client jolt should not automatically prove tissue stealing: the field study treated jolts as only potentially indicating cheating. [Caves, Chen & Johnsen (2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6769148/).

## Banded coral shrimp — *Stenopus hispidus*

**Observed daily activity and cleaning.** Continuous infrared recording at four Taiwanese reef stations produced 414 hours of footage. The thesis reported cleaning during only 5.6% of fish visits and 0.1% of visiting time. Animals mostly sheltered in crevices by day and frequently left their station at night. It reported no recruitment “dance” and no joint cleaning in the observed stations. These are strong reasons to avoid making every passing fish a client, but the percentages reflect those stations, species mix, and observation method; they should not be copied into the app as universal constants. [National Taiwan Ocean University field thesis](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/cgi-bin/gs32/gsweb.cgi/login?o=dnclcdr&s=id%3D%22110NTOU5270011%22.&searchmode=basic).

**Observed light response and parasite work.** Laboratory light-cycle tests found predominantly night-time locomotion. Weak light reduced the day/night contrast, so shade or twilight can permit daytime activity. In controlled grouper parasite trials, *S. hispidus* reduced on-fish ciliate and monogenean parasites only during night trials, while *L. amboinensis* reduced the monogenean during day and night trials. The paper also found daytime leech removal by *S. hispidus*, so “nocturnal” should describe a strong tendency, not an absolute lock. [Esaka et al. (2016)](https://waseda.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/effect-of-light-intensity-and-wavelength-on-diurnal-activity-of-t/); [Vaughan et al. (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-32293-6).

**Observed pairs, molts, and reproduction.** In a mate-recognition experiment, banded coral shrimp reacted differently to a former partner and a size- and sex-matched stranger, indicating individual recognition; chemical cues were proposed but not proven as the mechanism. A captive breeding study held twelve size-matched pairs at 26°C and 35‰ salinity. Mating followed the female’s molt, mean egg incubation was 22.8 days, and mean adult intermolt intervals were about 25–26 days. The researchers paired animals deliberately because aggression made unrestricted housing problematic. These laboratory intervals are useful scale markers, not a timer guaranteed at another temperature or diet. [Johnson (1977)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0003347277900173); [Reproductive-cycle study (2010)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0044848610003182).

**Inference for the simulation.** Make the banded shrimp’s default state sheltering near a crevice, with longer forays in darkness and occasional cleaning when a suitable client pauses at its station. Distinguish a familiar mate from an unfamiliar conspecific; permit aggression around the shelter without treating a meeting as an inevitable fatal fight. Put any reproductive event after a female molt, with egg incubation on a weeks-long scale. Do not copy the skunk shrimp’s front-leg rocking signal to this species merely because both are sold as “cleaner shrimp.” [National Taiwan Ocean University field thesis](https://ndltd.ncl.edu.tw/cgi-bin/gs32/gsweb.cgi/login?o=dnclcdr&s=id%3D%22110NTOU5270011%22.&searchmode=basic); [Johnson (1977)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0003347277900173).

**Transfer caution.** Field studies measured a few sites and local fish communities; breeding and parasite studies used fixed conditions and hosts. Use them for direction, sequence, and plausible time scales. Calibrate encounter rates, aggression, and survival against the actual reef tank before presenting them as predictions.
