# Shrimp motion detail: skunk cleaner (*Lysmata amboinensis*) and banded coral (*Stenopus hispidus*)

Research pass: 2026-10-13. Companion to [12_cleaner_shrimp_detail.md](12_cleaner_shrimp_detail.md), which covers cleaning ethics, molt and pair behaviour, and field counts. This note adds locomotion, appendage use, antennae and proportions. No YouTube used. Commons fetched serially.

Tags: **Observed** = stated in a source. **Inference** = my extrapolation or a comparative analogue.

## Corrections

1. **Stenopus has two pairs of antennae, not three.** ADW: "two pairs of long, white, hair-like antennae." [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Stenopus_hispidus/)
2. **Stenopus has three pairs of chelae** (pereiopods 1 to 3). Only the 3rd is enlarged and banded. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenopus_hispidus); [Bauer 1981](https://research.nhm.org/pdfs/31534/31534.pdf)
3. **Territory is larger than the existing notes say.** Wikipedia: 1–2 m diameter. ADW: never observed moving more than half a metre "unless disturbed." Replace the 15 cm radius in video/invert_banded_shrimp.md and the 50 cm roam radius it proposes with a 1–2 m territory and a 0.5 m typical excursion. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenopus_hispidus)
4. **The existing tail-flip values (0.3 s, 20–30 cm/s) are unsourced.** Caridean analogues: about 30 ms abdominal flexion in *Pandalus*, and mean escape speeds of 0.4–1.1 m/s in *Crangon*. Neither is *Lysmata*.
5. **Wikipedia's "10 pairs of walking legs" for *Lysmata* is wrong.** Decapods have five pereiopod pairs.
6. **The 3–8 week molt interval is secondary.** Both Wikipedia (Lysmata) and ADW (Stenopus) give it. No primary source found.

## 1. Walking

- **Which legs walk (Stenopus): not sourced.** **Inference:** pereiopods 4 and 5 walk, with chelae 1–3 held forward.
- **Enlarged 3rd cheliped raised, not walked on:** not sourced. **Inference:** carried raised or forward.
- **Gait and step frequency (both): no data.** Comparative hermit-crab work shows an alternating tripod gait (*Coenobita compressus*, search snippet). **Inference:** alternating or tripod-like, short stride, pauses. [Search snippet, not opened]
- **Body height, pauses:** ADW says Stenopus remains "in the same area for days, months or even years." **Inference:** long stationary holds, slow crawl between.

## 2. Pleopods and escape

- **Lysmata pleopods:** "used for swimming and brooding eggs" (Wikipedia). No beat rate or direction. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysmata_amboinensis)
- **Lysmata beat frequency:** no measurement found. The existing 6–8 Hz remains [L].
- **Analogues (not shrimp):**
  - Antarctic krill: 3 Hz hover, 6.2 Hz fast forward, 3.8 Hz upside-down. **Observed (krill).** Murphy et al. 2011, *Marine Biology*, via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metachronal_swimming).
  - Mantis shrimp (stomatopod): 3.6–13 Hz at 0.2–1.9 m/s, not purely metachronal, with a long phase lag from pleopod 1 to pleopod 5. **Observed (stomatopod).** [par.nsf.gov](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10343188)
  - Crayfish: pleopods move ventrally just before tail flexion. **Observed (crayfish).** Hunyadi et al. 2020, *JEB* 223, [DOI 10.1242/jeb.219873](https://movie.biologists.com/video/10.1242/jeb.219873/video-1).
- **Lysmata metachronal direction and phase:** no data. **Inference:** short anterior-to-posterior wave, with the stomatopod lag as the shape reference.
- **Lysmata forward swimming, how often:** no data. **Inference:** brief, short-range, with reverse as the escape.
- **Caridean tail flips:**
  - *Pandalus danae*: abdominal flexion about 30 ms, body accelerations above 100 m/s², rotations near 75°. **Observed.** Meyhöfer & Daniel 1989, *JEB* (search snippet, not opened).
  - *Crangon crangon* (11–69 mm): mean escape speed 0.4–1.1 m/s; cephalothorax and antennal scales take part; usually escapes on its side. **Observed.** [Arnott et al. 1998](https://pure.uhi.ac.uk/en/publications/tail-flip-mechanism-and-size-dependent-kinematics-of-escape-swimm/)
  - **Inference for *Lysmata*:** 20–40 ms flexion, body rolled, speed in tens of cm/s.

## 3. Antennae and antennules

- **Stenopus antennae:** long, white, longer than the body (ADW). Waved to advertise from the cave mouth. **Observed.** No rate.
- **Stenopus dance:** side-to-side, whipping the antennae, staying near home (Wikipedia). First seen in the lab. **Observed.** No Hz.
- **Stenopus idle sweep:** not sourced. Existing note says slow sweep. Keep as [L].
- **Lysmata antennae:** two pairs; front pair forked (Wikipedia). Length cited as 3–5 in, flagged citation-needed. **Observed (fork); length weak.**
- **Lysmata antenna tap and lash:** tapping without contact; "lashing" accompanies leg rocking (Caves et al. 2019, Table 2). **Observed.** [PMC6769148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6769148/)
- **Antennule flick rate:** no shrimp value. Spiny lobster 0.5–2 Hz (1997 study, snippet). A quoted review gives ~3 Hz (0.5–4 Hz) for lobsters, crabs, and crayfish, unconfirmed. **Observed (analogue, conflicting).**
- **Stenopus antennule grooming:** 3rd maxilliped cup brush cleans one long antennule at a time; 2nd maxillipeds sometimes rub the tips. **Observed.** [Bauer 1981, Fig. 2](https://research.nhm.org/pdfs/31534/31534.pdf)
- **Stenopus flagellum grooming:** 1st pereiopod carpal-propodal brush, flagellum pulled through; 2nd chela (sometimes 3rd) depresses it. **Observed.** [Bauer 1981, Fig. 3](https://research.nhm.org/pdfs/31534/31534.pdf)

## 4. Cleaning

- **Lysmata cleaning appendages:** not named in the primary paper. Comparative review: carideans groom antennules with the 3rd maxillipeds, antennae with the 1st pereiopods, and body with one pair of chelipeds. **Inference for *Lysmata*.** [Bauer 1981](https://research.nhm.org/pdfs/31534/31534.pdf)
- **Lysmata targets:** "ectoparasites or dead tissue" (Caves et al.). Mouth and gill cleaning on the eel (Commons). **Observed.**
- **Bout duration:** scoring window was up to 1 min; no bout times reported. **Observed (window only).**
- **Stenopus cleaning posture:** perches at cave or ledge mouth; "usually remain[s] in contact with the substrate when cleaning" (ADW). **Observed.**
- **Stenopus cleaning claws:** "three pairs of claws" remove parasites, fungi and damaged tissue (Wikipedia). Which claws: not stated.
- **Climbing onto client:** Lysmata cleans a hand (touch pool, Wikipedia). Fish climbing not sourced. **Inference.**

## 5. Feeding

- **Food to mouth, both species:** no kinematic data. **Inference:** claws bring food forward, maxillipeds pass it to the mouth.
- **Food stealing:** no source.

## 6. Stenopus specifics

- **Inverted hanging under ledges, claw threat display, backward retreat:** not sourced. **Inference.**
- **Claw loss:** claws break off under threat and regrow, often unequal (ADW). **Observed.**
- **Pairs:** monogamous; females larger; territory shared over years (ADW, Wikipedia). **Observed.**

## 7. Lysmata specifics

- **Side-to-side rocking dance:** "rocking dance from side to side to signal their services when fish approach" (Wikipedia). Caves et al.: "rapid back and forth rocking of the white front pair of legs" in 51% of interactions with predatory clients versus 12% non-predatory; followed by cleaning in 45% versus 5%. No Hz. **Observed.**
- **Rocking triggers:** dark stimuli and reduced light in lab trials. **Observed.** [PMC6769148](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6769148/)
- **Molt:** every 3–8 weeks (Wikipedia). **Observed (secondary).**
- **Nocturnal:** captive parasite removal "mainly at night" (Wikipedia). Vaughan et al. 2018: night and day in grouper trials. **Observed (mixed).**
- **Hanging on vertical rock:** not sourced; "caves or reef ledges" (Wikipedia). **Inference.**
