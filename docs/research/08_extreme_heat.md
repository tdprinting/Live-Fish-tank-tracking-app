# 08 — Catastrophic heat (33–40 °C): stuck-heater behavior, lethality, recovery

Scope: extends existing research (which stops at ~32–33 °C: gasping, appetite loss, hiding, Q10 ventilation, Pcrit rising with T, two-time-constant stress). This note covers the lethal band, 33 °C to ~40 °C, for a 60 gal (227 L) mixed reef. Values marked **est.** are my extrapolations from the closest family or from general CTmax methodology; everything else cites a primary source. WebFetch was unavailable in this environment, so numbers come from abstracts, repository records and dataset descriptions; tables in full papers were not read.

Headline for the owner's objection: **the literature agrees with them.** Surface-seeking is an oxygen behavior, not a heat behavior. In a well-aerated heated tank the dominant sequence is agitation, then slowing, then sinking to the bottom, loss of equilibrium on the side, spasms, death. Minutes to a few hours above ~36 °C, not "swim to the top and wait."

## 1. Thermal limits by group (CTmax, lethal temperatures, time-to-death)

### 1.1 Key methodological facts (apply to every row below)

- **CTmax** = temperature at loss of equilibrium (LOE) in a ramped trial. Standard ramp 0.3 °C/min (Becker & Genoway 1979), reef-fish labs often use 1 °C/h. It is an *incipient lethal* endpoint: fish removed at LOE usually survive; fish left at that temperature die within minutes.
- **First LOE vs CTmax**: Chung (1980), tropical marine fishes: CTmax 38–41 °C, first LOE 36–40 °C, gap 0.93–2.66 °C. Use CTmax − 1.5 °C as "first LOE" (est.).
- **Acclimation**: CTmax rises ~0.3–0.5 °C per 1 °C of acclimation temperature in fishes (e.g. convict/African cichlid 38.5 → 41 °C for 26 → 32 °C acclimation, McDonnell & Chapman 2015; ballan wrasse and *A. ocellaris* same direction). The *agitation* temperature does **not** shift with acclimation (McDonnell & Chapman 2015). All numbers below are normalized to a tank held at **26 °C** unless noted.
- **Chronic vs acute limits differ by ~4 °C**: *Acanthochromis polyacanthus* CTmax 37.1 °C (1 °C/h from 30 °C) but long-term mortality already at 33 °C (Habary et al. 2017); juveniles at 34 °C died in 8–15 days (Zarco-Perelló et al. 2012).
- **Hypoxia only lowers CTmax below ~35 mmHg (~22 % air sat.)** (Ern et al. 2017, *Chromis atripectoralis*, *Cheilodipterus quinquelineatus*, *A. polyacanthus*). Above that, CTmax is unaffected even though aerobic scope is gone. So in an aerated tank the heat death is a neural/cardiac failure, not suffocation.

### 1.4 Corals (acute pathway, distinct from DHW bleaching)

- *Stylophora pistillata* (northern Red Sea, unusually tolerant): no symbiont loss up to 32 °C over 7 h; **rapid tissue necrosis and mortality at 34.5 °C** within the 7 h exposure; Fv/Fm ED50 34.5 °C acute vs 33.8 °C chronic (Evensen et al. 2021).
- *Acropora cf. hemprichii*: **100 % mortality after 48 h at 32 °C** (untreated controls, KAUST probiotics study 2025); *Pocillopora verrucosa* survived the same with no tissue loss. Acropora ED50 is 0.4–0.7 °C lower than Pocillopora (Coral Sea CBASS study).
- Mechanism of fast death: heat shuts down symbiont photosynthesis, tissue becomes internally anoxic, then sloughs; "within hours to days"; ciliary motion collapses ~37 °C (bioRxiv 2024.10.15.618297 + Dryad dataset). Worse in the dark (no photosynthetic O2) — est. 1.5–2× faster at night.
- Field: 2023 Caribbean/Florida heatwave killed *Acropora* by direct tissue loss without a bleaching phase.
- Bottom line: above ~33–34 °C corals do not bleach first; they die by tissue sloughing, SPS (Acropora) first, Pocillopora/Stylophora next, LPS and soft corals last (est. +1–2 °C).

## 2. Behavioral sequence as temperature passes tolerance

Sources: Kochhann et al. 2021 (agitation temperature T_ag defined as leaving cover and swimming erratically, occurs several °C below CTmax); Nilsson et al. 2019 Atlantic salmon sudden warm transfer (high-speed swimming, wall collisions, head shaking from the start; **surface breaking, circling and lateral body bending only just before LOE**; LOE within 5 min at ≥28 °C for 8 °C-acclimated fish); rainbow trout LOE progression (tail drops first while the fish can still right itself, then it rolls and cannot recover); CTmax reviews (LOE = brain/heart dysfunction, loss of evoked brain responses, cortisol/lactate/K+ elevated at CTmax; heart rate falls at CTmax while ventilation stays high).

Stages, with triggers expressed as temperature relative to group CTmax (for ramps) and as fraction of lethal time (for a hold). Durations are for a stuck-heater ramp of ~1 °C/h (see §4).

| # | State | Trigger (ramp) | Trigger (hold at T) | What it looks like | Duration (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Chronic heat stress (existing research) | T > 30–32 °C | — | reduced appetite, hiding, faster ventilation | hours–days |
| 1 | **Agitation / escape** | T ≥ T_ag = CTmax − 4 (est.; agitation window 2–5 °C below CTmax) | first 0–25 % of lethal time | leaves cover, fast erratic darting, bumping glass/rock, "pacing" at edges, jumping (clowns, wrasses, blennies, gobies will jump), inverts climb toward waterline, shrimp swim in open water | 1–3 h on a 1 °C/h ramp |
| 2 | **Ventilatory max / gasping** | T ≥ CTmax − 3 | 25–50 % | opercular rate 2.5–3× baseline, mouth open, fins spread; still mobile; **stays mid-water or near rock, not at surface, unless O2 is low** (see §3) | 1–2 h |
| 3 | **Lethargy / sinking** | T ≥ CTmax − 2 | 50–80 % | activity collapses, fish settles on the bottom or wedges into rock, clamped fins, tail droops but can right itself, ventilation still high and shallow; shrimp stops cleaning and sits motionless; snails lose grip and drop; corals begin mucus/tissue strands | 1–2 h on ramp; this is the longest visible stage at a hold |
| 4 | **Loss of equilibrium (LOE)** | T ≥ CTmax − 1.5 (first LOE) to CTmax | 80–95 % | rolls on side, cannot right, drifts with flow, occasional burst (circling, body bending); brief surface-breaking bursts are possible here (salmon) but the fish does not hold station at the surface | 10–40 min at a hold near CTmax; seconds–minutes if T > CTmax + 1 |
| 5 | **Spasms / death** | T ≥ CTmax + 0.5–1 or any time > ~5–10 min at CTmax | 95–100 % | muscular spasms, gaping, then ventilation stops; heart failure | minutes |

Species adjustments: blennies/gobies/engineer goby skip visible stage 1 (they hide low rather than dart) and show stage 3 as "stays in burrow, head out, heavy ventilation"; hermits/Percnon express stage 1 as climbing out of water and stage 3 as falling off rock and lying inverted; shrimp express stage 4 as lying on side with twitching pleopods; tangs spend the shortest time in stage 3 (large active fish go from gasping to LOE fast).

## 3. Do fish go to the surface in a heated tank?

- **Surfacing (aquatic surface respiration, ASR) is triggered by O2 partial pressure, not temperature.** ASR thresholds across 31 tropical fishes: 6–40 torr PO2 (Kramer & McClure 1982), roughly 1.5–2 mg/L. Saturated 35-ppt seawater still holds ~6.6 mg/L at 26 °C, ~5.5 mg/L at 36 °C and ~5.1 mg/L at 40 °C, i.e. 3× the ASR threshold. An aerated tank therefore does **not** trigger ASR by heat alone.
- What heat does: raises the O2 demand and raises Pcrit and the ASR-onset PO2 (Lake Magadi tilapia: ASR threshold PO2 was 1.8× higher at 37.5 °C than at 31 °C, Narahara et al. 1996). So surfacing happens when heat is **combined** with reduced O2: pump/skimmer off, night (no photosynthesis; respiration of corals, algae, bacteria), decomposition of the first dead inverts, or a bacterial bloom. In a stuck-heater event these coincide often (overnight, first shrimp/snail deaths), so surfacing can appear as a *secondary* symptom around stage 2–3.
- **Thermal gradient works against surfacing.** A submerged heater drives convection; warm water accumulates at the surface and the sand/bottom and the return-flow path stay 0.5–2 °C cooler (est.). Fish behaviourally thermoregulate toward cooler microhabitat (Persian Gulf fishes shelter more at summer extremes), so the attractor is the **bottom, caves, sand bed, in front of the return**, not the top.
- Ern et al. 2017: CTmax is unchanged by hypoxia down to ~35 mmHg. Heat death is a neural/cardiac collapse, so the dying fish is on the bottom, not at the surface.
- Surface contact that does occur: (a) jumping/escape in stage 1; (b) brief surface-breaking bursts immediately before LOE (Nilsson 2019 salmon); (c) genuine ASR only if O2sat < ~25–30 % (est.; hot water raises the threshold). A fish that has reached stage 3 does not have the swimming capacity to hold at the surface; it sinks.

Implementation rule: `surface_seek` requires `O2_sat < O2_asr(T)` where `O2_asr(T) = 20 % + 1.5 %·(T − 28)` (est.), regardless of T. Heat alone drives the agitated → bottom → side sequence.

## 4. Ramp-rate effects

- **Stuck-heater physics (60 gal):** 227 kg × 4.18 kJ/kg/K = 950 kJ per °C. A 300 W heater adds 1.08 MJ/h → **~1.1 °C/h** with no losses (200 W → 0.75 °C/h; two 300 W → 2.3 °C/h). Room losses slow the climb and cap the plateau; a typical uncovered tank with a single 300 W heater plateaus around 34–38 °C (est.) after 10–15 h; a covered tank with 500 W+ exceeds 40 °C. So the real event is a **slow ramp of ~1 °C/h starting overnight**, reaching 33 °C in ~6 h and 36 °C in ~9 h from 26 °C.
- **Fast ramps (>0.3 °C/min) give higher measured CTmax** because damage needs time to accumulate (early-life reef fishes: faster heating → higher CTmax; general CTmax reviews). **Very slow ramps give lower CTmax** because the fish exhausts itself at sub-lethal temperatures (Mora & Maya 2006: blenny tolerance peaked at ~1 °C/h and fell at both faster and slower rates). Net: for the sim, treat CTmax tables as valid for 0.5–2 °C/h; apply −1 °C (est.) for ramps slower than 0.3 °C/h (multi-day creep, e.g. summer heat + weak chiller) and +1 °C (est.) for spikes faster than 10 °C/h (but then the hold-time table dominates anyway).
- **Sudden spike** (hot top-off, heater in a small sump dumped into display): salmon transferred 8 → 28+ °C lost equilibrium within 5 min with no lethargy stage; stages 1 → 4 compress to minutes. Model: if dT/dt > 5 °C/h, skip stage 3's duration (set to 10 % of normal).
- **Slow climb** gives the full visible sequence and partial acclimation: chronic stress (existing model) runs first, appetite loss and hiding precede agitation by hours; sessile inverts and corals die *before* fish (shrimp and Turbo/Trochus at 34–35 °C, Acropora at 33–34 °C), so the first observable deaths in a real stuck-heater event are the cleaner shrimp, the snails and the Acropora, then cardinals, then tangs/clowns, then the dottyback/hawkfish, with blennies, engineer goby, nerites and hermits last.

Damage integrator (recommended): `D += dt / t_death(T_eff)` where `t_death` is the §1.5 curve for the group, `T_eff = T − T_refuge_offset` (0.5–1 °C for bottom-dwellers in a stratified tank, est.), D only accumulates for T > CTmax − 4, and decays at a slow rate below that (§6). Stage = f(D) and f(T − CTmax) whichever is further along.

## 5. Corals under extreme heat vs DHW bleaching

Two separate pathways, both run in parallel:

1. **DHW / bleaching (existing):** slow, weeks, symbiont expulsion, pale → white, recoverable if < ~8 DHW.
2. **Acute necrosis (new):** engages when T > T_necro (SPS Acropora 33 °C; Pocillopora/Stylophora/Montipora 34 °C; LPS Euphyllia/Favia 34.5 °C; soft corals/zoanthids 35.5 °C; all est. from Evensen 2021, KAUST 2025 and the O2-deprivation preprint). The tissue goes anoxic and sloughs: visual sequence is heavy mucus (first 20 % of t_sloough), polyp retraction and brown/grey tissue strands peeling in the flow (20–70 %), bare white skeleton spreading from tips/edges (70–100 %). No "bleached" phase. Darkness accelerates ×1.5–2 (no photosynthetic O2). Sloughed tissue fouls the water (ammonia, O2 drop), feeding §3's secondary hypoxia.
3. Night-time acute death is the common real-world story: heater sticks after lights-out; by morning the Acropora has sloughed and the shrimp is dead at 34–35 °C while most fish are still alive but lethargic.

## 6. Recovery when temperature drops back

Evidence: LOE in CTmax trials is survivable when fish are removed promptly (zebrafish 99 % survival over repeated trials, Morgan et al. 2018; but an earlier zebrafish study saw low survival in 30 °C-acclimated fish, i.e. small margin between LOE and death when already warm-acclimated); rainbow darter back to routine MO2 ~3 h after heat-LOE; sturgeon plasma glucose/lactate/osmolality back to control within 24 h and CTmax repeatable after 24 h; capture-stress heart-rate recovery takes >24 h in a third of salmon; delayed mortality in fish stress studies concentrates in the first 24 h. Cooling itself is a stressor (hobby guidance: ≤1–2 °C/h).

| Stage reached | Outcome if T returns to ≤30 °C | Recovery time (est.) | Delayed mortality (est.) | Residual effects (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 agitation | full | 1–3 h (fast cortisol constant) | 0 % | none; appetite back same day |
| 2 gasping | full | 3–12 h | 0–2 % | appetite suppressed 1–2 d; immune/disease susceptibility up for 1–2 weeks (ties to existing disease model) |
| 3 lethargy on bottom | usually survives | 6–24 h to swim normally | 5–15 % over 72 h | no feeding 1–3 d; D decays with τ ≈ 24 h; CTmax temporarily +0.5 °C (heat-hardening) for ~1 week |
| 4 LOE (on side) | survives only if cooling starts within ~5–10 min of LOE (est.) | 1–3 h to right itself, 24–48 h to normal | 25–50 % over 24–72 h; higher if warm-acclimated already | fin/skin damage, secondary infection |
| 5 spasms | dies | — | >90 % | — |
| Shrimp at stage 3–4 | poor; moult often triggered and fails | — | 50 % at stage 3, >90 % at stage 4 | |
| Snail detached (stage 3–4) | 50/50; if it does not re-attach within ~12 h it is dead | | | dead snails must be removed (ammonia) |
| Coral partial sloughing | the remaining tissue survives if < ~30–40 % lost (est.) and T drops below T_necro within hours; no regrowth over bare skeleton for weeks; algae colonizes bare skeleton | | colony death if > 50 % lost | |

Cooling-rate rule: dropping faster than ~3 °C/h (est.) adds a second shock; the sim should treat a fast chill after heat as a small added stress (not lethal by itself at these temperatures), while > 1–2 °C/h is already an aggressive hobbyist rescue.

### 7.2 Behavior state machine (fish)

```
HEAT_NORMAL        T < T_ag                                   existing chronic-stress behaviours still apply above T_chronic
HEAT_AGITATED      T ≥ T_ag  or  D ≥ 0.05                      speed ×1.8, leaves shelter, random-walk edges, jump chance ↑ (clown/wrasse/blenny/goby), ignores food
HEAT_GASPING       T ≥ CTmax−3  or  D ≥ 0.25                   vent rate = min(3×base, Q10 curve), speed ×1.0 then declining, drifts toward coolest voxel (bottom / return flow), no feeding
HEAT_LETHARGIC     T ≥ CTmax−2  or  D ≥ 0.50                   speed ×0.2, target = bottom/cave, posture: clamped fins, tail-down, vent still 3×, occasional righting
HEAT_LOE           T ≥ CTmax−1.5  or  D ≥ 0.85                 on side, physics = passive body carried by flow, random 1–3 s burst every 30–90 s (may breach surface), vent 2×
HEAT_DEATH         T ≥ CTmax+1  or  D ≥ 1.0                    spasm animation 10–60 s, then dead; corpse decomposition adds NH3 and consumes O2
SURFACE_SEEK       (overlay) only if O2_sat < 20 % + 1.5 %·(T−28); available only in AGITATED/GASPING; in LETHARGIC the fish cannot hold station at the surface
```

State is the max of the T-trigger and the D-trigger; transitions down (recovery) happen only via D decay (τ_decay = 24 h once T < CTmax − 4) and never faster than one state per 2 h, except AGITATED → NORMAL (30 min). Delayed-mortality roll at D peak: P = 0 below 0.5, 0.10 at 0.5–0.85, 0.35 at 0.85–1.0, applied over the following 48 h. Shrimp: same machine with LETHARGIC = motionless on rock, LOE = on side with twitching; mortality roll 0.5/0.9. Snails: AGITATED = moving up glass, LETHARGIC = loss of attachment (falls), DEATH after t_death; re-attach chance 50 % if cooled within 12 h. Hermit/Percnon: AGITATED = climb to waterline (T_eff reduced by refuge offset while emerged, capped at 6 h out of water before desiccation stress applies). Corals: `D_coral` integrates only above T_necro with night ×1.7; visual stages mucus (0.2), strands (0.2–0.7), bare skeleton fraction = clamp((D−0.7)/0.3); irreversible: tissue fraction lost persists after cooling, colony death if lost > 0.5.

### 7.3 Acceptance checks

1. **Stuck 300 W heater, lights off, from 26 °C, no O2 problem** (O2_sat stays > 80 %): tank climbs ~1 °C/h. Expected log: cleaner shrimp and Turbo snails LETHARGIC by ~32–33 °C (~6–7 h) and dead by ~35 °C (~9 h); Acropora sloughing begins by 33–34 °C and > 50 % skeleton exposed by 36 °C; cardinalfish LOE at ~34.5–35 °C, dead ≤ 36 °C; clownfish/tang LETHARGIC on the bottom at ~34.5 °C, LOE ~35–36 °C, dead by ~37 °C; blenny, engineer goby, hermits, nerites still alive at 37 °C. **No fish enters SURFACE_SEEK at any point.** Fail if any fish is at the surface when T > 34 °C with O2_sat > 60 %, or if any fish survives 30 min at CTmax + 1.
2. **Same event but pump off (O2_sat falls to 25 % by 33 °C):** clownfish and tang enter SURFACE_SEEK while AGITATED/GASPING (31–34 °C), then **leave the surface and sink** when they reach LETHARGIC. Fail if a LETHARGIC or LOE fish is holding position at the surface.
3. **Sudden spike 26 → 38 °C in 10 min** (hot water dump): every fish reaches LOE within 2–10 min and death within 15 min for cardinals/clowns/tangs and 30 min for blennies/gobies; the LETHARGIC stage lasts < 2 min (no bottom-sitting plateau). Fail if any fish shows > 10 min of LETHARGIC before LOE.
4. **Rescue at stage 3:** ramp to 34.5 °C, clownfish LETHARGIC (D ≈ 0.6), then cool at 1.5 °C/h to 27 °C. Expect: swimming normal within 6–24 h, not feeding for 1–3 d, 5–15 % death probability over 72 h, CTmax +0.5 °C for 7 d. Shrimp in the same run (D ≈ 1 at 35 °C) is dead. Fail if clownfish is feeding within 6 h or if more than 20 % of replicate runs kill the clownfish.
5. **Rescue at stage 4:** hold a dottyback at 37 °C until LOE (~1 h), cool immediately (within 5 min). Expect righting in 1–3 h, 25–50 % death over 48 h. Hold at 37 °C for 15 min after LOE instead: death in ≥ 90 % of runs. Fail if post-LOE survival exceeds 75 % or if the 15-min hold survives.
6. **Corals vs DHW path:** hold 31.5 °C for 10 days → bleaching only (pale, no tissue loss, recoverable). Hold 34.5 °C for 12 h → Acropora shows mucus by 1 h, strands by 2–3 h, > 50 % bare skeleton by 6 h, and never shows the "bleached white with tissue" state; Pocillopora reaches strands by ~5 h; soft corals only retract. Night run is ≥ 1.5× faster than day run. Fail if a coral at 34.5 °C passes through the pale-bleached state before sloughing.
