# Reef microfauna catalogue: gaps and corrections (ReefLive)

Scope: reef-aquarium microfauna and small cryptic life only. Companion to `micro_fauna.md` (behaviour per MicroGroup) and `/mnt/project-files/reeftank/research/10_microfauna.md` (groups, populations, model). This note adds only kinds or facts missing from those.

Tags: **[M]** cited to a URL below (S-number). **[L]** estimate, no source. **[H]** not used: no video was watched (YouTube blocked; Wikimedia Commons clips not pulled, so no frame-level evidence exists yet).

Method: skimmed the existing headings and the `MicroGroup` enum, then ran 11 web searches for the gap candidates. Pages were mostly not opened; claims are snippet-level. Most sources are hobbyist or retailer pages.

## 1. Already covered (not repeated)

Harpacticoid, Calanoid ("Tigger pod"), Cyclopoid, Gammarid, Caprellid, Mysid, Ostracod, Isopod, Rotifer, Ciliate, Phyto, Bristleworm, Micro-snail, Spaghetti worm, Foraminifera, Red flatworm (Planaria). Research 10 also covers Stomatella/Collonista, Convolutriloba, Terebellid worms and Eurythoe.

## 2. Gap kinds (new)

### 2.1 Spirorbid tube worms (Family Spirorbidae)
- Size: [L] 1-5 mm tube diameter, calcareous spiral shell; feeding crown small.
- Look: white or cream coiled tubes on glass, pumps, plumbing and rock. Crown of tentacles only when feeding.
- Movement: sessile. Crown extends and retracts; no walking.
- Day/night: crown open by day when feeding [L]; no source on night behaviour.
- Habitat: glass, powerheads, plumbing, live rock; prefers high flow [M, S1].
- Diet: suspended fine particles [M, S1].
- Population: forum reports of booms that fade over months, sometimes sudden rises [M, S1, S2]. No density published.
- Status: harmless filter feeder per hobbyists; control is mechanical scraping; less fine food may limit outbreaks [M, S1].

### 2.2 Vermetid worm snails (Family Vermetidae)
- Size: [L] shell 5-20 mm; tube length varies.
- Look: irregular tube-snail on rock and coral; mucus net strands visible as sheets when wet.
- Movement: sessile. Casts a mucus net for 20-40 min, then reels it in and swallows [M, S3].
- Day/night: net retraction rate varies with time of day [M, S4].
- Habitat: rock and coral heads, wave-exposed zones in the wild [M, S4].
- Diet: plankton and particles trapped in mucus nets. Net about 10 cm across around the individual [M, S4].
- Population: fast reproduction, few predators, manual removal [M, S3, S5]. No density data.
- Ecological effect: mucus nets trap plankton and shade nearby coral, reducing coral growth [M, S4]. (Specific % figures seen only in search summaries were dropped as unsourced.)
- Verdict: pest. Eaten by [L]: bumblebee snails and emerald crabs (anecdote only) [M, S5].

### 2.3 Hydroids (e.g. Aglaophenia, Pennaria)
- Size: colony height [L] 1-10 cm; individual polyps about 1-3 mm.
- Look: feathery or plume-like colonies of branches on rock, macroalgae and sponges [M, S6, S7].
- Movement: sessile colony; polyps extend tentacles to feed.
- Day/night: no source.
- Habitat: rock, macroalgae, sponges [M, S7].
- Population: hobbyist pest; control advice is manual removal and treatment that avoids other livestock [M, S6]. Some Aglaophenia are flagged as toxic or unknown toxicity [M, S7].
- Eaten by: sea spiders (pycnogonids) feed on hydroids in nature [M, S8].
- Verdict: pest or hitchhiker.

### 2.4 Pycnogonids (sea spiders)
- Size: [L] 2-10 mm body; long legs. Smaller than small-mouthed coral polyps in some reports [M, S9].
- Look: spindly, pale or coloured body, very long thin legs; "praying" stance [L].
- Movement: slow walk on coral and rock; sits on prey.
- Day/night: no source.
- Habitat: coral and zoanthid colonies, rock [M, S9].
- Diet: sessile animals: zoanthids, corals, anemones; most species eat hydroids [M, S8, S9]. Feeding on fire coral and zoanthids documented in Great Barrier Reef field work [M, S8].
- Eaten by: not reported in aquarium sources [gap].
- Verdict: pest on zoanthid and coral frags. Hard to remove; dips and manual removal recommended, effectiveness disputed [M, S9].

### 2.5 Tunicates (sea squirts, Didemnum)
- Size: colony 1-5 cm [L].
- Look: encrusting or mat-like colonies; purple, pink, green or yellowish [M, S10].
- Movement: sessile.
- Diet: plankton, fine dried food [M, S10].
- Status: most Didemnum listed as not suitable for aquarium keeping; some have no captive success and some toxicity unknown [M, S10]. Usually arrive as stowaways on live rock [M, S10].
- Verdict: hitchhiker, low sim priority [L].

### 2.6 Sycon calcareous sponge (Pineapple or Q-tip sponge)
- Size: up to about 7.5 cm (wiki max) [M, S11]; hitchhiker size usually small [L].
- Look: white or cream tube or vase-shaped, on live rock in shade [M, S11, S12].
- Movement: sessile filter feeder.
- Diet: filter feeding on suspended particles [M, S12, hobbyist].
- Status: most common aquarium sponge; harmless. Other sponges can be invasive and harder to remove [M, S12].

### 2.7 Micro brittle stars (small ophiuroids)
- Size: [L] disc 3-10 mm; arms longer.
- Look: small starfish with thin arms, usually brown or white; only seen at night or in crevices [M, S13].
- Movement: arms crawl; disc lifts; stirs sand [M, S13].
- Day/night: nocturnal; hide in rock and substrate by day [M, S13, S14].
- Habitat: rock crevices, sand [M, S13].
- Diet: scavenging uneaten food, detritus [M, S13].
- Population: vendor claims they breed in most aquaria (marketing, not verified) [M, S13].
- Eaten by: hermit crabs can damage them [M, S14].
- Verdict: beneficial detritivores. Note: green brittle star (Ophiarachna incrassara) can eat fish, so do not treat all brittle stars as one kind [M, S14].

### 2.8 Chitons (Acanthopleura, Acanthochitona)
- Size: [L] 1-5 cm (fuzzy chiton larger; not measured in sources).
- Look: oval plate-armoured body, girdle with spines or fuzz; grey, brown, green [M, S15].
- Movement: slow crawl on rock; grips hard when disturbed [L].
- Day/night: nocturnal grazer in one retailer note [M, S15].
- Habitat: rock and algae.
- Diet: film and macroalgae grazing [M, S15].
- Status: conflicting. One retailer says hardy but a bulldozer; German database says not kept successfully and Acanthochitona not suitable [M, S15, S16].
- Verdict: grazer, possibly beneficial. Low priority for sim [L].

### 2.9 Siphonaria (false limpets) and Cellana (true limpets)
- Siphonaria: max shell about 25 mm (1 inch) [M, S17]. Grazes film algae; tolerates salinity 20-40 ppt [M, S17]. Captive-bred stock sold [M, S17]. Pulmonate snail, not a true limpet [M, S17].
- Cellana: scrapes micro-algae with radula [M, S18]. Aquarium success not recorded in German database [M, S16].
- Grazing: where Cellana graze they can remove all algae; Siphonaria leave a thin film [M, S19].
- Sim: Siphonaria fits a grazer kind with a larger size than MICROSNAIL. Cellana low priority.

### 2.10 Convolutriloba retrogemma (pest acoel flatworm)
- Size: sources disagree: 2 mm (profile), 5-7 mm (hobbyist), under 15 mm (retailer) [M, S20, S21].
- Look: flat, rust-red to orange, with posterior lobes; carries algal symbionts (zooxanthellae) [M, S20, S22].
- Movement: glides on mucus/cilia [L].
- Diet: algae, detritus; also rotifers and copepods in one profile [M, S20].
- Reproduction: asexual fission and regeneration; sexual reproduction rarely seen in genus [M, S21].
- Population: explodes with high nutrients; blankets corals and blocks light [M, S22]. Possibly feeds on coral zooxanthellae, not established [M, S22].
- Control: siphon, Flatworm Exit; dying worms may release toxins; copper harmful [M, S22].
- Verdict: pest.

### 2.11 Berghia nudibranch (Aiptasia predator)
- Size: [L] 5-15 mm; not sourced.
- Look: pale aeolid with cerata; gills can look brown from ingested zooxanthellae [M, S23].
- Movement: slow crawl; night hunter, hides in crevices in light [M, S23].
- Diet: Aiptasia only; starves without prey; needs anemone several times weekly [M, S23, S24].
- Predators: night shrimp, crabs, some wrasses [M, S23].
- Verdict: beneficial biocontrol. Taxonomy disputed. Efficacy mixed [M, S23, S24].

## 3. Corrections to existing notes

1. **Tigger pod species.** `micro_fauna.md` says Tigger = *Tigriopus* (harpacticoid). Research 10 says Tigger = *Parvocalanus* (calanoid, 0.2-0.4 mm). The sim CALANOID size of 0.3 mm matches Parvocalanus. Keep CALANOID as Parvocalanus, relabel it, and move Tigriopus to the harpacticoid set. Both species are in trade, so this is a labelling call [L].
2. **FLATWORM label.** Current sim FLATWORM is Red Planaria. Convolutriloba behaves differently (algal symbionts, fission, nutrient-driven blooms, coral smothering). Split into a separate kind.
3. **Aiptasia.** Not microfauna in the sim sense; covered by `docs/research/video/coral_aiptasia.md`. Only the Berghia predator belongs here.
4. **Fireworms** (Hermodice) were in the requested list but are macro-scale and not in the sim. Excluded.

## 6. Priority fixes for Opus

1. Split CALANOID vs Tigriopus (section 3.1). Keep the 0.3 mm size for Parvocalanus.
2. Add NEW kinds for Convolutriloba, Vermetid, Spirorbid, Hydroid and Pycnogonid. These are the pest kinds a reef keeper would most want to see.
3. Add NEW kinds for Micro brittle star and Berghia, the beneficial ones with a clear diet. Brittle stars come out at night (S13, S14).
4. Vermetids and hydroids should be sessile with a net or stalk; no walking in the sim [M, S4, S22].
5. Sizes and speeds for all NEW kinds are [L]. Do not present them as sourced until a video or measurement confirms them.
