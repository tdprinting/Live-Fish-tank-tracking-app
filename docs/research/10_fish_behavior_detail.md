# Fish behavior: species evidence and observation guide

Research update: 2026-10-12. Scope: the nine fish taxa represented in ReefLive, including the two ocellaris color forms. This adds behavioral depth to [02](02_fish_species.md) and [04](04_more_species.md). It is research, not an implementation claim or a set of experimentally validated simulation constants.

## How to read the evidence

- **Direct study:** an experiment or field observation on the named species. Its conditions and population matter.
- **Species account:** a museum, breeder, or curated database account. Useful for identification and natural history; not equivalent to an experiment establishing a rate.
- **Analogy:** evidence from another species. The transfer is explicitly identified.
- **Research proposal:** an observation or modeling idea suggested here, not a measured biological result.

FishBase links are curated species accounts that identify their underlying references. Where an original experiment is available, it is linked separately. Access was checked on the date above; an abstract-only result is used only for claims visible in that abstract. Numbers from a study are not aquarium defaults. Total length (TL), standard length (SL), age, reproductive phase, and captive versus wild setting must remain distinct.

## 1. Ocellaris clownfish — Amphiprion ocellaris

### Social structure and reproduction

**Species account.** The largest member of a group is the breeding female; the next largest is the breeding male, with smaller nonbreeders below them. Loss of the female can lead to the male becoming female and another fish taking the male role. This is a social succession process, not an instantaneous change whenever one fish is removed. The museum account also describes an omnivorous diet including small crustaceans, zooplankton, and algae. Orange and black color forms should not receive different aggression rules merely because their colors differ. [Florida Museum species profile](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/discover-fish/species-profiles/clown-anemonefish/).

**Analogy with an explicit limit.** A clownfish acoustic study distinguished dominant charges/chases from subordinate lateral head-shaking. Its detailed submissive-sound measurements were on *A. frenatus*, not the tank's *A. ocellaris*. It supports distinguishing threat, yielding, and injury in an ethogram; it does not establish an ocellaris quiver frequency or an aquarium hourly chase quota. A quiver can be a social response rather than a disease sign. [Colleye & Parmentier, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3492317/).

### Host association

**Direct captive experiment.** Juvenile ocellaris were reared with different anemone hosts in a study of host choice and growth. Host identity and developmental context belong in any interpretation of attachment to a particular shelter. This does not show that all soft corals are equivalent to natural anemone hosts, or that the owner's Kenya tree must always be occupied. [Host choice and fitness experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6850181/).

**Research proposal.** Record each clown separately: time near the chosen host, who initiates a chase, who yields, and whether both resume feeding afterward. Distinguish a brief displacement from persistent exclusion from food or shelter. Document nest cleaning, egg presence, and tending only when observed; a paired status alone should not trigger egg production. For animation reference, capture lateral quivers separately from forward swimming and turning. No fixed quivers/hour, host radius, or pairing success percentage is established by these sources.

## 2. Palette/blue tang — Paracanthurus hepatus

### Feeding and refuge use

**Species account.** This species occurs on current-swept reef terraces and is reported in loose aggregations above the bottom. Juveniles and subadults associate with branching coral and retreat tightly into branches when alarmed. Its reported natural diet includes zooplankton and occasional algae; treating it as a lawnmower-style obligate surface grazer loses an important difference. FishBase lists a maximum of 31 cm TL, not a guaranteed size for every individual. [FishBase: P. hepatus](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Paracanthurus-hepatus.html).

**Research proposal.** Separate open-water food interception, optional surface picking, transit between feeding areas, and refuge entry. Measure the actual fish's turning space and refuge access as it grows instead of converting tank volume into a hard biological growth cap. Record body orientation in a refuge before categorizing an unusual resting pose as illness. A useful observation distinguishes a reversible alarm retreat from prolonged hiding accompanied by missed meals.

### Cleaner interactions and limits of extrapolation

**Related-species experiment.** Moving mechanical cleaner models lowered cortisol in *Ctenochaetus striatus*. The design isolated tactile stimulation from parasite removal. It provides evidence for a possible benefit of physical contact in a surgeonfish, but not a measured stress-decay constant in *P. hepatus*, nor evidence that one cleaner halves aggression across a whole mixed tank. [Soares et al., 2011](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1547).

**Research proposal.** Log whether the tang voluntarily approaches, poses, leaves, or jolts during a cleaning encounter. Keep contact time, parasite removal, and subsequent behavior as separate observations. A color change alone should not be labeled a specific emotional state without repeated within-fish observations under consistent lighting.

## 3. Pajama cardinalfish — Sphaeramia nematoptera

### Daily rhythm and breeding

**Species account.** FishBase describes daytime aggregations among branching *Porites* in sheltered bays and lagoons, dispersal at night to feed near the bottom, mouthbrooding, and distinct pairing during courtship/spawning. These are a strong basis for different daytime sheltering and nighttime foraging behavior. They are not evidence for total daytime immobility or complete refusal of prepared food in daylight. [FishBase species account](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/sphaeramia-nematoptera); [reproduction record](https://www.fishbase.se/Reproduction/FishReproSummary.php?GenusName=Sphaeramia&ID=5778&SpeciesName=nematoptera&StockCode=6072&fc=304).

**Research proposal.** Measure distance from cover at several lighting stages, including the ramp down, rather than changing behavior at a clock label alone. Record a feeding attempt separately from a successful bite. Compare the same individual before and after a nearby territorial fish approaches. Use repeated observation to determine whether a narrow daytime range reflects normal refuge use, social pressure, or a recent introduction.

**Evidence gap.** This pass did not establish a species-specific numerical shade preference, exact mouthbrooding duration under this tank's conditions, or a physiological threshold for when it must become more exposed. Do not import Banggai cardinalfish reproductive timing into pajama cardinalfish simply because both mouthbrood. Expanded buccal appearance would be an observation to investigate, not proof of disease or a breeding event.

## 4. Bluestreak cleaner wrasse — Labroides dimidiatus

### Cleaning is a negotiated interaction

**Species account.** Stations can be occupied by adults or juveniles in different social arrangements. Food includes ectoparasites and client mucus. Dance-like movements can precede an encounter. The current species account lists 14 cm TL maximum, differing from the older 11 cm value in research 02; preserve the source and metric rather than silently treating either as an aquarium target. [FishBase species account](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Labroides-dimidiatus.html).

**Direct experiment.** A service-quality study found different responses to cortisol manipulation in wrasses from socially complex versus simpler habitats. Cleaner behavior can depend on experience and the social setting, not just hunger or client size. The paper's experiment does not establish a universal cheating probability for every aquarium wrasse. [Reputation management study, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28827634/).

**Direct experiment.** A neuro-molecular study related cleaning interactions to immediate-early gene expression and recorded tactile stimulation during its trials. Behavioral measurements and neural associations are different levels of evidence: they do not make a single observed jolt a complete account of client trust. [Neuro-molecular characterization, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9119974/).

**Research proposal.** Use an encounter sequence for observations: approach, advertisement, client acceptance/pose, inspection or tactile pass, withdrawal or jolt, and later re-approach. Include failed approaches in the denominator. A client swimming past without stopping is not a completed cleaning visit. Record accepted prepared foods independently of cleaning; the presence of clients is not itself a measurement of adequate energy intake.

**Unresolved quantities.** This research does not supply aquarium-wide service guarantees, client-specific inspection quotas, starvation deadlines, or a fixed trust decrement. Record duration distributions and individual histories before fitting them. Avoid turning field service frequencies into a guarantee that every client must be touched within 90 minutes.

## 5. Falco hawkfish — Cirrhitichthys falco

**Species account.** FishBase describes resting at coral-head bases and thickened, elongated lower pectoral rays. It reports haremic reproductive organization, occasional pairs, and pelagic spawning; cited spawning ascents span 0.2–0.6 m. The listed maximum is 8 cm TL, which differs from the earlier 7 cm value. [FishBase: dwarf hawkfish](https://www.fishbase.org/summary/5445).

**Research proposal.** Build an observation record around perch occupancy, head/eye orientation, departure, short transit, and landing. A perched fish should not need to be motionless in every appendage. Count perch changes separately from feeding strikes and social charges. The available species account does not establish “5–20 hops per hour,” a mandatory 75% perch budget, or a universal prey cutoff of half the hawkfish's length.

**Compatibility uncertainty.** For any shrimp encounter, record shrimp size and molt condition, available cover, whether the hawk was feeding, and whether contact actually occurred. An approach, failed strike, injury, and kill are different outcomes. Do not substitute observations from flame hawkfish for this species without labeling the transfer. Do not add nightly spawning to a solitary animal just because it occurs in wild reproductive groups.

## 6. Lawnmower blenny — Salarias fasciatus

### What “grazing” removes

**Direct study.** A feeding and dental study found that *S. fasciatus* removed both photosynthetic material and organic biomass from an experimental surface film. The reported reductions were 57% and 38.5%, respectively, under those experimental conditions. This reconciles detritus-rich gut contents with a real effect on algae: the fish works on an epilithic matrix containing more than algae alone. It does not justify removing 57% of an aquarium's algae every meal. [Christiansen, Kemp & Tibbetts, 2010, author-shared paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226757000_Are_the_oral_teeth_of_combtooth_bennies_Salariini_Teleostei_merely_combs).

**Direct field study.** At Heron Island, territorial blennies chased approximately 90% of entering blennies but about 20% of entering damselfishes. That is an encounter-conditioned result in a particular community, not a 90% chance that any nearby tailspot will be attacked at every simulation tick. [Ecological significance study, university repository](https://research.usc.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-ecological-significance-of-the-combtoothed/99548308102621).

**Research proposal.** Separate grazing-site quality from visible hair-algae cover. Record bite bouts, travel between patches, shelter use, and displacement of competitors. For aggression, count intrusions first, then responses, with distance, identity, and whether the intruder left. For feeding, note whether prepared food was swallowed, mouthed and rejected, or never reached. These observations are more informative than a single all-fish hunger score after surface feeding.

## 7. Tailspot blenny — Ecsenius stigmatura

**Species account.** FishBase lists 6 cm TL maximum and records adults alone or in small groups in sheltered reef and lagoon habitats. Identification details include a dark spot at the caudal-fin base, a yellow iris, and a yellow line beneath the eye. Eggs are attached to the substrate; larvae are planktonic. These details address an explicit coloration gap in research 04. [FishBase: tailspot coralblenny](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Ecsenius-stigmatura.html).

**Research proposal.** Distinguish head-out shelter inspection, a short feeding excursion, return to shelter, and full retreat. A favored hole and the fish's exposed head position should be recorded separately. The presence of a larger blenny suggests a competition question worth observing, but does not establish that the tailspot must move exactly 50 cm away or remain hidden for a prescribed fraction of daylight.

**Evidence gap.** Species-specific chase probabilities, feeding displacement by *S. fasciatus*, and recovery time after relocation were not established here. Do not transfer the lawnmower's experimental diet percentages to this smaller genus. Repeat observations across more than one day before concluding that one hiding episode is a permanent territory choice.

## 8. Indigo dottyback — Pseudochromis fridmani × P. sankeyi

**Breeder's primary account.** ORA identifies the Electric Indigo as this hybrid and distinguishes its appearance from the earlier Indigo line. It reports color variation with condition and describes the line as relatively peaceful among dottybacks. This is direct breeder experience, not a controlled compatibility trial. The project's `pseudochromis_indigo` identifier is an application label, not evidence that *Pseudochromis indigo* is a valid species name. [ORA Electric Indigo profile](https://www.orafarm.com/product/electric-indigo-dottyback/).

**Research proposal.** Record the individual's cave, patrol route, retreats, feeding location, and approaches to each neighbor. Track whether an encounter was at the cave entrance or in open water. Treat the owner's actual hybrid identity as provisional until its purchase record or appearance confirms the line. ORA's characterization is a reason to avoid assigning extreme aggression from unrelated dottyback species, not a guarantee of peaceful coexistence.

**Evidence gap.** No direct evidence found here establishes that this hybrid must attack a new cardinal at least three times in its first hour or stop after seven days. Such numbers remain model choices. A useful longitudinal record compares the same dyad at introduction, after several days, and after a change in shelter layout, without intentionally provoking attacks.

## 9. Engineer/convict fish — Pholidichthys leucotaenia

**Museum account.** Despite the trade names “goby” and “blenny,” this animal belongs to Pholidichthyidae. Juveniles have a pale longitudinal stripe on a dark body, and the species excavates substantial tunnel systems. The museum's account specifically identifies larval and juvenile holdings; it should not be treated as a quantitative adult feeding study. [Australian Museum](https://publications.australian.museum/blog-archive/science/bbc-life-convict-fish-footage/).

**Research context.** A 2026 phylogenomic paper describes the unusual burrow-associated life history and emphasizes unresolved reproduction. Suggestions that juveniles provision adults remain hypotheses in that discussion. They should not become a settled juvenile-to-parent food-transfer mechanic. [The origin of cichlids: Pholidichthys phylogenomics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1055790326000515).

**Research proposal.** Separate juvenile open-water activity from adult burrow-associated behavior, but leave the age/size transition adjustable until longitudinal observations exist. Record excavation as substrate pickup, transport, and release; distinguish that sequence from feeding. Map the entrance actually used, secondary exits, and sheltered paths. Measure whether food near the entrance is taken before interpreting failure to chase surface food as reduced appetite.

**Evidence gap.** This pass does not validate a hard 15 cm transition, an 18 cm feeding radius, or a 5% nightly patrol probability. Those may be useful provisional model settings, but their provenance must remain “design estimate.” The animal should not inherit snowflake-moray predation or body behavior merely because both have elongated shapes.

## 10. Questions that need tank observations

This is a proposed observation protocol, not a request to alter husbandry or induce stress. Use ordinary activity and routine feeding already taking place.

| Question | Record | Avoid confusing it with |
|---|---|---|
| Who loses access to food? | Successful bites, rejected items, displacement, food location | Simply failing to visit the surface |
| Is a fish resting or excluded? | Location, later emergence, neighbor approaches, routine feeding response | One still frame of hiding |
| Does cleaning change later behavior? | Contact duration and subsequent re-approaches for the same pair | A universal tank-wide calming effect |
| Does light change activity? | Actual light ramp, shade, exposure, and time | A binary clock label |
| Is a territory stable? | Entrance, usable shelter, patrol path, encounters across days | A fixed coordinate chosen by software |
| Is an interaction escalating? | Approach → display → chase → contact → injury as separate events | Treating every approach as an attack |

For useful counts, define the denominator: attacks per intrusion, cleaning acceptances per approach, bites per accessible food item. Keep failed and interrupted interactions. Record fish identity, approximate length, light state, and observation duration. Repeat routine observations on different days. Report ranges and individual variation rather than inventing precise biological constants from one clip.

## 11. Corrections and unresolved claims in earlier notes

The additions above should qualify older sheets when they conflict. They do not silently edit existing implementation values.

- *P. hepatus* is not interchangeable with a generic herbivorous tang; its species account emphasizes zooplankton.
- *S. fasciatus* grazing acts on a mixed surface matrix, so visible hair algae alone is not a full food budget.
- Indigo is a hybrid label; aggression from another *Pseudochromis* species is an analogy.
- Engineer fish is not a moray, true goby, or true blenny.
- Museum/database maximum lengths are reference records, not tank-size targets or guaranteed growth endpoints.
- Species temperature ranges are not lethal thresholds, and this file supplies no new heat-death, salinity-death, or starvation timers.
- Predetermined hourly quotas in acceptance tests are hypotheses to calibrate, not newly verified findings from these papers.
