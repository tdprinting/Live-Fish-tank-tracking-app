# Ocellaris clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris): reproduction and early development

Scope: numbers for a reef-tank simulation. Sources are listed per number. Several figures conflict between studies; the conflicts are flagged. Items with no source found are marked GAP.

## 1. Spawning
- Spawning interval: 12-15 days (Madhu, Madhu & Retheesh 2012, 7 captive pairs, year-round) https://www.mbai.org.in/uploads/manuscripts/article%2010.pdf ; 12-14 days (Raheem et al. 2021, black morph) https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJF/article/view/102354 ; 12-15 days, mean 24 (+/-4) clutches per pair per year (Roux et al. 2021) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8248105
- Lunar: spawning clustered near full moon, fewer during waning moon (2022 Brazilian study of 563 Amphiprion spawnings; exact URL and A. ocellaris-only sample not verified). Animal Diversity: spawning concentrated around full moon, usually in the morning, citing Thresher 1984 https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/amphiprion_ocellaris/
- Time of hatching: about 1 h after lights off (Roux 2021); 19:00-20:00 (Raheem 2021).
- Temperature: rearing studies used 26-28.5 C. Roux 2021 used 26 C; Madhu 2012 used 28.5 C; Yasir & Qin (2007) used about 27 C (cited in the Vietnamese embryology paper, https://vjs.ac.vn/jmst/article/view/6284). Optimum not established by these sources.
- Clutch size: 100-500 eggs (Roux 2021); 300-1000 (Madhu 2012); first spawn of young pairs 200-300, later spawns larger (Raheem 2021). Clutch size rises with female age and size; no per-size table found. GAP for min/typical/max by female size.
- Egg size: 1.5-3.0 mm long x 0.8-1.84 mm wide (Madhu 2012). Oocytes 3.0-3.3 mm x 1.1-1.9 mm (Raheem 2021). Yasir & Qin 2007: about 2.59 +/- 0.14 mm long. The shape is capsule-like and adhesive (Madhu 2012).
- Attachment: eggs are glued to a rock substrate in a flat clutch, not stalked (Roux 2021, Madhu 2012).
- Egg colour by day (search summary, source not confirmed): pale yellow or white days 1-2; black days 3-5; silver (eyes visible) days 6-7. Source: Madhu 2012 (via search snippet only; verify).
- Incubation: 168 h (7 d) at 28.5 +/- 0.5 C (Madhu 2012); 7-8 d (Raheem 2021; Yasir & Qin 2007 at 27 C); about 10 d at 26 C (Roux 2021). Rule of thumb: about 7 d at 28 C, 8-10 d at 26 C.
- Male and female care: male prepares the nest on bare rock beside the anemone; courtship is fin extension, biting and chasing (Fautin & Allen 1992, via Animal Diversity). Males court and defend more aggressively during spawning. Female is larger (Animal Diversity). Nocturnal care in the congener A. percula: both parents fan at night, and females provide more care at night (BU study, https://open.bu.edu/items/f929deb6-2947-47ef-b05e-e3727e076cfd). Fanning by the male during the day is widely reported; the sources above do not give the rate.
- GAP: fanning and mouthing rates in min/hour.

## 2. Larvae
- Length at hatch: 5.0 +/- 0.16 mm total length (Raheem 2021); 3.63 +/- 0.1 mm (Yasir & Qin 2007 as summarised); about 4 mm (breeder record). Use 4-5 mm as the range.
- Mouth and yolk: mouth open at hatch or day 7 of embryo (Yasir & Qin 2007 as summarised; the yolk wording is unclear). Feeds right after hatch. GAP: yolk absorption day in a reliable source.
- Pelagic duration and metamorphosis: Raheem 2021 reports metamorphosis to juvenile on day 40 at 18.87 +/- 0.07 mm, which conflicts with breeder logs. Breeder logs for A. ocellaris: metamorphosis at day 7-10 (one record), 9 d, 10 d (breedersregistry.org, hobby). Aquarium summary (coralmagazine): 10-15 days. Conflict: use 10-15 d as a hobby rule and 40 d as a hatchery upper bound. GAP: settlement length in peer-reviewed data.
- Feeding schedule: rotifers (Brachionus) from day 1-2 for the first days; Artemia nauplii start at day 5-6. Setiawati 2008 (Indonesian J.): Artemia from day 6 gave the highest survival (about 81%), vs 62% at day 10 and 67% at day 15, with no growth difference (https://journal.ugm.ac.id/jfs/article/view/8946). Murray et al. 2024 (Aquaculture America, abstract): transition to Artemia possible at day 5; microdiet from day 5 gave the best survival (https://www.was.org/Meeting/Program/PaperDetail/162060). Raheem 2021 cultured rotifers, copepod nauplii and Artemia. Density figures (10-20 rotifers/mL) were not found in these sources; GAP.
- Survival in captivity: 39% to juvenile with enriched Brachionus and Artemia, 11% with enriched rotifers and unenriched Artemia, and no larvae past day 7 with unenriched live prey (Olivotto et al. 2006, but this study is on the sunrise dottyback, not clownfish, https://iris.univpm.it/handle/11566/52707). Olivotto et al. 2011 on A. ocellaris: HUFA enrichment is limiting for development and metamorphosis; no numbers retrieved (title confirmed). Unfed survival: GAP for A. ocellaris.
- Greenwater and algae: Yasir & Qin 2007 larvae were fed rotifers and algae for 5 days. Greenwater use is hobby practice; no peer-reviewed number found.
- Why larvae die in display tanks: predation by fish and corals, pump and filter intake, and lack of food. No quantitative source found; GAP.

## 3. Metamorphosis and juveniles
- First white head bar: about 7-11 d after hatch (breeder records; head bar seen at day 7-8, metamorphosis at day 7-10, all bars by day 20-25). Source: breedersregistry.org (A. ocellaris AMPOCE-041897-KERRIS-006). Hobby data, not peer-reviewed.
- Bar order: head first, then mid-body, then tail (research summary, https://news.cnrs.fr/node/1399). Colour change: from pale or transparent larva to orange during metamorphosis; hobby records.
- Growth: juveniles fed 2-8% body mass per day (Roux 2021). 3-4 cm at 6-8 months for well-fed fish (hobby source, akwarium.gdynia.pl). Day-40 length 18.87 mm (Raheem 2021; this is at metamorphosis, not juvenile growth). GAP: monthly lengths at 1, 2, 3, 6, 12 months from peer-reviewed data.
- Maturity: a new pair first spawns 6 months to 2 years after pairing (Roux 2021). Broodfish began courtship after about 1.7 years (Raheem 2021). Size at maturity: GAP (females larger than males; no mm figure).
- Juvenile behaviour: juveniles stay near the anemone and the group. Constant fighting among juveniles in one tank caused mortality up to 90% (Roux 2021). Breeding pair suppresses maturation of subordinate juveniles, and the largest can take over after the female dies (Buston 2003, cited in Roux 2021). Pair aggression toward juveniles: not confirmed in these sources.

## 4. Larval morphology (for 3D modelling)
- Body length 4-5 mm at hatch (Section 2). Eyes large relative to head; pigment and fin fold details: GAP (Roux et al. 2019 staging table, https://hal.sorbonne-universite.fr/hal-02272895, was blocked by bot protection).
- Transparency: larvae are translucent at hatch and gain orange and bar pigment at metamorphosis (hobby and summary sources).
- Swimming: pelagic, drifts in the water column; no peer-reviewed description of hover or dart mode found. GAP.
- Yolk sac: oval at hatch (saddleback congener, A. polymnus, study). Not verified for A. ocellaris.

## 5. Waste and food intake
- Juvenile and adult feeding: 2-8% body mass per day (Roux 2021). Larval intake: GAP. Ammonia from larvae: GAP.

## Key conflicts and gaps
- Metamorphosis day: 7-11 d (hobby breeder logs), 10-15 d (aquarium summary), 40 d (Raheem 2021). A simulation should use 10-15 d as default with a hatchery option of up to 40 d.
- Hatch length: 3.6 mm vs 4-5 mm. Use 4.5 mm as a midpoint.
- Egg length: 1.5-3.0 mm (Madhu) vs oocytes 3.0-3.3 mm (Raheem). Use 2.5-2.6 mm for eggs.
- Gaps (no source found): yolk absorption day, settlement length, monthly juvenile growth, age and size at maturity, larval morphology details, larval survival unfed, egg colour source, Ghosh and Avella papers (not found in search).
- Unverified (cited only in search snippets): 2022 Brazilian lunar study.
