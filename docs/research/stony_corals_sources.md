# Reef coral husbandry research (13 corals)

Compiled 2026-10-20. Sourcing notes:

- Most husbandry numbers in the hobby come from retailer care sheets (Top Shelf Aquatics, Bulk Reef Supply/ORA, Fragbox, Reef Chasers, Gensou, Queen City Corals). These are marked "retail". Where two retailers disagree, both values are listed.
- Reef Builders, TFH Magazine (Goemans) and NOAA Coral Reef Watch are the strongest non-retail sources reached. Peer-reviewed material was found only for bleaching thresholds, Acropora field growth, Xenia pulsing, Heteroxenia photosynthesis and brown-jelly ciliates.
- Borneman "Aquarium Corals" and Reefs.com/Advanced Aquarist articles were searched for but could not be fetched (403/429 errors). Nothing below is attributed to Borneman unless he is named in a fetched page.
- Values marked "estimate" were not found in any source reached. Values marked "not sourced" have no number in any source reached.
- Unit conversions used: 75 F = 23.9 C, 76 F = 24.4 C, 77 F = 25.0 C, 78 F = 25.6 C, 80 F = 26.7 C, 82 F = 27.8 C. Alkalinity: 1 meq/L = 2.8 dKH.
- Taxonomy: several hobby names are out of date. Euphyllia ancora and E. divisa are now Fimbriaphyllia ancora and F. divisa (paradivisa likewise); Acanthastrea lordhowensis is Micromussa lordhowensis (genus assigned 2016). The older names are still common in the trade.

## 1. Hammer coral - Fimbriaphyllia (Euphyllia) ancora

- Temperature: optimum 75-80 F (24-26.7 C) [retail]. Bleaching onset: not sourced (estimate: about 1 C above local summer max, see section 0).
- PAR: retail disagrees. One guide says 75-150 PAR, starting low and ramping over 1-2 weeks. Bulk Reef Supply branching-hammer listings say 150-250 PAR. Estimate: 75-200 PAR, low-medium light.
- Flow: low to moderate, indirect. Too much flow tears tissue; too little invites detritus.
- Alkalinity 8-9.5 dKH; calcium 400-450 ppm; magnesium 1250-1350 ppm; SG 1.025-1.026 [retail].
- Nitrate/phosphate: not sourced. Hard-skeleton corals need Ca/alk dosing as they grow.
- Growth: not sourced in a number. Described as moderate.
- Spread: polyps extend; colonies grow by budding and branching of the colony; branching varieties form tight clusters [retail, BRS].
- Feeding: photosynthetic primarily; target feeding of mysis, brine or reef roids 1-2x/week is optional but helpful [retail].
- Aggression: sweeper tentacles present, reach not sourced in a number. Retailers advise 6-8 in (15-20 cm) spacing. Estimate: sweepers extend at night to roughly 5-10 cm.
- Chemical warfare: none noted. Stings nearby corals by sweeper contact.
- Look and movement: long fleshy tentacles that sway in flow; tentacles retract under strong flow. Pulse rate: not applicable.
- Sizes sold: frags 1-2 in with 2-4 heads typical [retail].
- Prices (2025-26 US): not verified for this species. Estimate: $40-$120 frag, $100-$300 colony.
- Colors: tan, green, purple, neon/branching varieties. Fluorescence: many morphs fluoresce green-yellow under actinic/blue light [retail, BRS "almost glows under actinic lighting" was said of a different ORA variety, so treat as general].
- Failure modes: brown jelly disease, tissue recession from high flow or unstable Ca/alk, flatworms are not a main problem.
- Difficulty: moderate. Popular, beginner-friendly for an LPS if stable.

Sources: [RETAIL-Hammer-QueenCity] https://www.queencitycorals.com/?p=95131883 ; [RETAIL-Hammer-Bulk] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ecc-aurelia-branching-hammer-eye-catching-coral.html ; [RETAIL-Hammer-Bulk2] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ecc-polar-plunge-branching-hammer-eye-catching-coral.html

## 2. Torch coral - Euphyllia glabrescens

- Temperature: 76-80 F (24.4-26.7 C) [retail]. Bleaching onset: not sourced (estimate: as section 0).
- PAR: 100-200 PAR (one guide) [retail]. Estimate: 100-200.
- Flow: moderate, indirect. One guide warns a hard blast cuts tissue from the skeleton and causes recession.
- Alkalinity 8-10 dKH; SG 1.025-1.026; pH 8.1-8.4 [retail]. One guide: swings above 1.0 dKH in 24 h commonly trigger recession in Euphyllia (weak source).
- Calcium 400-450 ppm; magnesium 1250-1350 ppm [estimate, consistent with other Euphyllia].
- Nitrate/phosphate: not sourced.
- Growth: not sourced in a number. Reproduces by fission when mature, also by polyp budding. Not sourced otherwise.
- Feeding: photosynthetic. Small meaty foods (mysis, chopped shrimp) occasionally [retail].
- Aggression: sweeper tentacles reported 1-4 in (2.5-20 cm) in one source. Another source says up to about 25 cm and that strong current produces more sweepers. Retail spacing 6-10 in. Sweepers extend at night. Chemical warfare: none.
- Look and movement: long oral tentacles with sway; extends sweepers at night.
- Sizes sold: frag with 2-4 heads, typical 1-2 in [retail].
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: frag $60-$200 depending on morph; colony $150-$400. Top Shelf listed TSA Blowtorch and similar named morphs, but the Top Shelf Blowtorch page could not be fetched (429).
- Colors: green, gold, orange, banana, multiple morphs. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: brown jelly disease; tissue recession from flow or chemistry swings; very aggressive sweepers if placed too close.
- Difficulty: moderate; popular.
- Warning: one Top Shelf page mislabels the species as Fimbriaphyllia glabrescens.

Sources: [RETAIL-Torch-Gensou] https://gensou.sg/torch-coral-euphyllia-glabrescens-care/ ; [RETAIL-Torch-Fishkeeper] https://www.fishkeeper.co.uk/fish/marine/hard-corals-lps/torch-coral ; [RETAIL-Torch-TSA] https://topshelfaquatics.com/pages/corals/tsa-blowtorch-torch-coral (429 on fetch; from search summary)

## 3. Frogspawn - Fimbriaphyllia (Euphyllia) divisa

- Temperature: 75-80 F (23.9-26.7 C) [retail]. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- PAR: 50-150 (one source), 75-150 with ramp-up (another), 80-150 (third) [retail]. Estimate: 75-150.
- Flow: gentle, indirect; tentacles sway without being pressed flat.
- Alkalinity 8-10 dKH; calcium 400-450 ppm; magnesium 1250-1400 ppm; pH 8.1-8.4; SG 1.025-1.026 [retail].
- Growth: not sourced in a number.
- Feeding: photosynthetic. One source suggests small meaty foods 1-2x/week when tentacles are extended.
- Aggression: sweeper tentacles reach about 6 in (15 cm) beyond the coral; retail buffer 6-8 in. One source labels it AGGRESSIVE; another semi-aggressive. Extended at night, 10 cm or more.
- Look and movement: long, thick, sinuous tentacles that extend heavily at night.
- Sizes sold: frags typically 1-2 in with 2-4 polyps [retail].
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $50-$200 frag.
- Colors: green, blue, yellow, orange, pink tips. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: brown jelly, recession, sudden shifts in light.
- Difficulty: moderate; popular.

Sources: [RETAIL-Frogspawn-Gensou] https://gensou.sg/frogspawn-coral-euphyllia-divisa-care/ ; [RETAIL-Frogspawn-QueenCity] https://www.queencitycorals.com/?p=95131877 ; [RETAIL-Frogspawn-ReefChasers] https://reefchasers.com/blogs/reef-chasers-coral-care-guide/frogspawn-coral-care-guide

## 4. Duncan coral - Duncanopsammia axifuga

- Temperature: 76-80 F (24.4-26.7 C) [retail]. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- PAR: retail gives 75-150 PAR, starting low and raising gradually. Another source gives medium light.
- Flow: medium; can tolerate higher flow. Too much direct flow causes retracted heads and tissue recession [retail].
- Alkalinity: sources disagree. Estimate: 8-10 dKH. Calcium: estimate 400-450 ppm. Magnesium: not sourced.
- pH 8.1-8.4; SG 1.025-1.026 [retail].
- Nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: not sourced in a number. Described as easy, moderate. Spreads by budding new heads from the colony edge; grows as clusters of heads on a single base.
- Feeding: optional but recommended. Mysis, enriched brine, coral powders 1-3x/week [retail].
- Aggression: short nighttime sweepers; retail advises 3-4 in (7-10 cm) room for new heads and sweepers. Bristleworms nip at extended tentacles at night. Chemical warfare: not sourced. Sensitive to copper; copper medications are toxic [retail].
- Look and movement: long, flowing, whisker-like tentacles that sway; extended during the day, retracted under strong flow.
- Sizes sold: 1-2 in frags with 2-4 heads; ORA sells 1 in with 3-5 heads [retail].
- Prices (2025-26 US): Bulk Reef Supply ORA Duncanopsammia listing shows $80.65 and $94.88 (sale price unlabeled), for approx. 1 in with 3-5 heads. Estimate: $60-$150 frag; colonies $150-$400.
- Colors: green, blue, purple, pink, yellow. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: brown jelly disease when water quality drops suddenly; recession from strong direct flow.
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly LPS [retail].

Sources: [RETAIL-Duncan-BRS] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ora-duncanopsammia-coral-ora.html ; [RETAIL-Duncan-TSA-guide] https://topshelfaquatics.com/blogs/news/how-to-care-for-duncan-corals-a-complete-guide ; [RETAIL-Duncan-Fragbox] https://fragbox.ca/duncan-coral/

## 5. Acan - Micromussa lordhowensis (formerly Acanthastrea lordhowensis)

- Temperature: 75-78 F (23.9-25.6 C) [RB-Acan]. Top Shelf lists 76-80 F. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- PAR: retail 50-100 PAR (one retailer) [search summary]. Estimate: 50-125. Warned that high flow can stop full extension or tear the tissue.
- Flow: low to moderate.
- Alkalinity 8-12 dKH [RB-Acan]; Top Shelf 8-9.5. Calcium 400-450 ppm; magnesium 1250-1350 ppm [RB-Acan]. SG 1.025-1.026 [RB-Acan].
- Nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: described as "faster growing" relative to tank mates [RB-Acan]. No number.
- Feeding: target feeding small meaty foods (mysis, brine) 2-3x/week [RB-Acan]. Gensou suggests 1-2x/week.
- Aggression: sweeper tentacles can sting nearby corals; Reef Chasers says it is sometimes aggressive. Sweeper length not sourced.
- Look and movement: polyps extend fully when fed and under gentle flow; large polyps.
- Sizes sold: frags of about 3 cm with at least 4 polyps (Charterhouse) [retail].
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $30-$80 frag.
- Colors: green, blue, pink, bright eye variants (Bright Eye morph on Top Shelf). Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: not sourced beyond recession from flow and calcium/alkalinity instability.
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly; popular.

Sources: [RB-Acan] https://reefbuilders.com/2025/02/17/introduction-to-acan-micromussa-lordhowensis-care ; [RETAIL-Acan-Gensou] https://gensou.sg/acan-coral-micromussa-lordhowensis-care/ ; [RETAIL-Acan-ReefChasers] https://reefchasers.com/blogs/reef-chasers-coral-care-guide/micromussa-lordhowensis-coral-care-guide

## 6. Candy cane - Caulastrea furcata

- Temperature: not sourced in a number. Estimate: 75-80 F (24-27 C).
- PAR: 80-150 (one source), 50-250 wide range (another), 100-150 (Reef Chasers), 150-250 (ORA medium) [retail]. Estimate: 100-150.
- Flow: low to moderate, indirect. Extends feeding tentacles in gentle current and retracts under strong flow. Sensitive to strong flow and direct metal halide.
- Alkalinity, calcium, magnesium: not sourced in a number. Estimate: 8-10 dKH, 400-450 ppm Ca, 1250-1350 ppm Mg.
- Growth: not sourced.
- Feeding: targeted feeding with meaty foods like mysis [retail].
- Aggression: sweeper tentacles reach neighbors; give room. Chemical warfare: none noted.
- Look and movement: fleshy polyps; feeding tentacles extended at night; color can wash out under intense light and nutrient imbalance.
- Sizes sold: frags 1-2 in with 1-3 heads [BRS].
- Prices: Bulk Reef Supply ORA candy cane listing shows $28.80 and $33.88 for 1-2 in frag with 1-3 heads (sale unlabeled). Estimate: $25-$60 frag.
- Colors: green, pale green, tan, purple-tipped variants. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: pale color from light or nutrients; tissue loss from strong flow.
- Difficulty: easy, beginner-friendly.

Note: vendors use "trumpet coral" for C. echinulata as well; naming is inconsistent.

Sources: [RETAIL-Candy-BRS] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ora-candy-cane-coral-ora.html ; [RETAIL-Candy-ReefChasers] https://reefchasers.com/blogs/reef-chasers-coral-care-guide/candy-cane-corals ; [RETAIL-Candy-Gensou] https://gensou.sg/?p=41057

## 7. Open brain - Trachyphyllia geoffroyi

- Temperature: 23-28 C (74-83 F) [TFH-Goemans]. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- Light: moderate. Red morphs need shade or indirect light; green, tan and brown morphs tolerate low to moderate direct light [TFH-Goemans]. No PAR number given. Estimate: 50-150 PAR (retail-level, gensou gave 50-150 umol but was not corroborated).
- Flow: gentle to moderate, indirect [TFH-Goemans; Reef Builders].
- Alkalinity: 3.5 meq/L, which is about 9.8 dKH [TFH-Goemans; conversion mine]. Calcium 380-430 ppm; pH 8.1-8.2; SG 1.024-1.026 [TFH-Goemans].
- Magnesium: not sourced.
- Nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: not sourced in a number. Described as solitary or occasionally colonial. Spreads by budding and sometimes splitting when fed.
- Feeding: once per week at most. "More seems to cause odd growth forms" [TFH-Goemans]. Target the feeding tentacles with meaty foods.
- Aggression: the fleshy tissue abrades on rough rock. Clearance on all sides needed so tissue can expand [Reef Builders via search summary]. Sweeper length not sourced; estimate short.
- Look and movement: large fleshy disc; tentacles extend at night and in gentle flow.
- Sizes sold: small free-living specimens 1-3 in; larger for display.
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $50-$200 small, more for large morphs.
- Colors: red, green, tan, brown; "Wellsophyllia" forms are treated as morphs of this species by Fatherree [search summary, Reefs.com not fetched].
- Failure modes: tissue damage on rock; overfeeding distorts growth; sudden light increase bleaches red morphs.
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly per fragbox.ca; mostly sand-bed placement.

Sources: [TFH-Goemans] https://www.tfhmagazine.com/articles/saltwater/trachyphyllia-geoffroyi ; [RB-Trach] https://reefbuilders.com/2013/07/19/beginner-lps-coral-trachyphyllia-geoffroyi-open-brain ; [RETAIL-Trach-Fragbox] https://fragbox.ca/coralwiki/open-brain-coral-care/

## 8. Bubble coral - Plerogyra sinuosa

- Temperature: 23-28 C (74-83 F) per TFH; a guide gives 25-27 C. Sustained temperatures above 28 C leave vesicles partly deflated [search summary, TFH and a retail guide].
- Light: 50-150 PAR (one guide), 100-150 (Fragbox), 100-200 (retail). Bubbles shrink under intense light. Acclimate slowly [RB-Bubble; retail].
- Flow: very gentle, turbulent water movement is best [RB-Bubble].
- Alkalinity: not sourced. Calcium 380-440 ppm; magnesium 1300-1400 ppm; SG 1.025 [TFH and retail].
- Nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: not sourced in a number.
- Feeding: "actively fed at least a few times a week" [RB-Bubble]. Photosynthetic share not sourced; one source notes it needs supplemental feeding.
- Aggression: long sweeper tentacles armed with stinging nematocysts at night; Fragbox lists temperament as aggressive [RB-Bubble; RETAIL-Bubble-Fragbox].
- Look and movement: bubbles expanded during the day; contract at night to reveal tapered feeding tentacles [RB-Bubble].
- Sizes sold: small frag with 1-2 bubbles, to large colonies. Not sourced in inches.
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $40-$150 for a frag, depends on bubble count.
- Colors: green, pink, red, yellow, bouncy variants. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: skeleton is fragile; easy to cut or sever when placed on rock; shrinkage under high light.
- Difficulty: beginner per RB-Bubble and Fragbox; rugged.

Sources: [RB-Bubble] https://www.reefbuilders.com/2014/02/17/bubble-coral-plerogyra-sinuosa-beginner-friendly ; [RETAIL-Bubble-Fragbox] https://fragbox.ca/coralwiki/bubble-coral-care/ ; [TFH-Plerogyra] https://tfhmagazine.com/articles/saltwater/plerogyra-sinuosa ; [Reefs.com Borneman] https://reefs.com/?p=209318 (403 on fetch, not read)

## 9. Montipora capricornis (plating)

- Temperature: 75-79 F (24-26 C) [retail]. Range also quoted as 72-78 F and 77-82 F. Stability matters more than the exact number. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- PAR: 150-250 PAR (most sources); 200-350 (one); 150-300 (one). Lower PAR gives flat, broad plates; higher PAR gives tight plates. Ramp over 2-3 weeks.
- Flow: moderate, indirect. Plates can act as a sail; strong flow lifts the colony off the rock. Avoid direct jets at growing edges.
- Alkalinity 8.0-9.5 dKH; calcium 400-450 ppm; magnesium 1250-1450 ppm; SG 1.025-1.026 [retail].
- Nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: fast-growing; one guide says a calcium reactor, kalkwasser or dosing may be required for Montipora.
- Feeding: mostly photosynthetic; phytoplankton or rotifers 1-2x/week helpful [retail].
- Aggression: not sourced. Plating corals are not known for sweepers.
- Look and movement: plates or leaves, polyps visible at the surface, not large.
- Sizes sold: small frag 1-2 in plate; colony larger [retail].
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $15-$60 frag.
- Colors: green, purple, red, pink, blue; pink rim varieties. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: lifting off rock from flow; bleaching if light ramped too fast; tissue recession under swings.
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly; widely sold.

Sources: [RETAIL-Montipora-Gensou] https://gensou.sg/montipora-capricornis-care-guide/ ; [RETAIL-Montipora-BRS] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/wwc-spearmint-montipora-cap-world-wide-corals.html ; [RETAIL-Montipora-TSA] https://topshelfaquatics.com/pages/corals/red-montipora-cap-coral

## 10. Birdsnest - Seriatopora hystrix

- Temperature: 76-80 F (24.4-26.7 C) per ORA; a Top Shelf listing says 77-82 F [retail]. Bleaching onset: not sourced.
- PAR: ORA lists medium 150-250 and high 250-350+; Top Shelf/ORA medium to high [retail]. Estimate: 150-300.
- Flow: medium to high. Important as the colony grows; outer branches block flow to the interior [retail].
- SG 1.025-1.026; pH 8.1-8.4; alkalinity 8-12 dKH; calcium 380-450 ppm; magnesium 1200-1400 ppm [ORA, retail].
- Nutrients: not sourced in ORA. Estimate: nitrate low, phosphate near zero, per general SPS practice.
- Growth: retail labels differ: Fragbox "Very Fast" in one listing, "Fast" in another; ORA "Moderate" but "grows relatively fast, quickly regenerating any broken branches" [retail]. No measured rate found for aquaria.
- Field/transplant study (Bali, 2021): 98.3% survival of transplanted fragments; vertical planting grew faster than horizontal; 5 cm and 7 cm fragments grew faster than 3 cm, and 5 cm was recommended as starting size [BIRDS-Bali].
- Mesocosm study: salinity stress lowered growth; growth recovered slowly after return to 35 PSU [BIRDS-Bali, search summary].
- Feeding: photosynthetic. Not sourced otherwise.
- Aggression: none noted. Branches are delicate.
- Look and movement: branching, many tips; polyps extend in flow.
- Sizes sold: 1-2 in frags with 1-3 heads [BRS].
- Prices (2025-26 US): Bulk Reef Supply ORA pink birdsnest listing shows $38.15 and $44.88 (sale unlabeled) for 1-2 in, 1-3 heads. Estimate: $25-$80 frag.
- Colors: purple, pink, hyacinth, green. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: tissue loss when water quality changes; branch breakage; RTN or tissue recession from high nutrients.
- Difficulty: moderate. Fragbox calls it fairly hardy when stable and one of the easier SPS, but also delicate.

Sources: [RETAIL-Birdsnest-BRS] https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ora-pink-birdsnest-coral-ora.html ; [RETAIL-Birdsnest-Fragbox] https://fragbox.ca/product/purple-cactus-birdsnest/ ; [BIRDS-Bali] https://doaj.org/article/6df5aaca44e5400ea42f39f69bf95a02 (Omni-Akuatika, Jun 2021; the study itself is at https://acadstaff.ugm.ac.id/karya_files/the-growth-of-seriatopora-hystrix--dana--1846--transplant-with-differences-of-fragments-size-and-planting-position-dcfcd07e645d245babe887e5e2daa016)

## 11. Acropora (A. millepora, A. tenuis)

Growth (wild, peer-reviewed or field): 
- A. cervicornis about 37 cm/yr and A. prolifera about 31 cm/yr of branch extension, tagged branches, southwest Puerto Rico, one year; growth much higher in winter and spring [PMC7020822, search summary].
- A. palmata, Curacao: monthly extension about 0.6-0.8 cm in 1971-73 and 7-11% slower in 2002-04 (Bull Mar Sci 2009, Coral Growth Rates Revisited after 31 Years) [ingentaconnect URL in appendix].
- A. humilis, Red Sea: 7-9 mm/yr, depending on exposure [search summary; study not fetched].
- Aquarium-specific growth rates were not found. Estimate for aquaria: fast branching species several cm/yr; millepora and tenuis roughly 2-5 cm/yr under good light and flow.

Per-species:

A. millepora
- Temperature: 76-80 F (24.4-26.7 C) [retail]. Bleaching onset not sourced.
- PAR: 250-400 (Top Shelf), 250-350 (second Top Shelf). Gensou calls it the most forgiving beginner acro and says it tolerates alkalinity swings better than most [retail].
- Flow: strong, turbulent, varied [retail].
- Alkalinity: 8-9.5 dKH, held steady [retail]. Calcium 420-460 ppm; magnesium 1300-1400 ppm [retail].
- Nitrate: 2-10 ppm; phosphate 0.02-0.08 ppm (Top Shelf). Tip burn follows high PAR with high alkalinity and low phosphate [retail].
- Growth: described as fast [retail].
- Feeding: photosynthetic; not sourced otherwise.
- Aggression: none. Sweepers none.
- Look and movement: branching table or staghorn; polyps retract at night.
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $30-$100 frag.
- Colors: blue, green, purple, pink, cyan tips. Fluorescence: strong green or orange fluorescence under actinic light in many morphs [retail].
- Failure modes: tip burn, STN, Acropora-eating flatworms (AEFW).
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly SPS.

A. tenuis
- PAR: 250-400 PAR for best color; upper end needed for strong fluorescence [Gensou].
- Other parameters as for A. millepora. Nitrate: 5-15 ppm and phosphate 0.03-0.08 ppm for color (Top Shelf second listing); the German reeflex database says nitrate under 5 mg/L and phosphate under 0.1 mg/L, ideally about 0.01 mg/L phosphate [reeflex].
- Growth: described as fast. Most vendors recommend a 2-3 week light ramp.
- Difficulty: mid. Harder than millepora, easier than tortuosa.

AEFW (Amakusaplana acroporae): 1-3 mm translucent flatworm; eats tissue and leaves circular white patches; eggs and bite marks at the base of the colony. Host range broad; prefers A. valida (2013 study) [search summary, Springer 2013 and 2011 studies]. Survives 5-7 days out of host (2013 study), so quarantine and an isolation period of at least a week are the usual advice [search summary]. Dips in Lugol or Revive for 5-10 min repeated every 4-7 days are hobbyist advice; not peer-reviewed.

Sources: [RETAIL-Acro-Gensou-millepora] https://gensou.sg/?p=32620 ; [RETAIL-Acro-Gensou-tenuis] https://gensou.sg/?p=32622 ; [RETAIL-Acro-TSA-millepora] https://topshelfaquatics.com/pages/corals/rmf-acid-trip-millepora-acropora-coral ; [RETAIL-Acro-TSA-tenuis] https://topshelfaquatics.com/pages/corals/tgc-acrolandia-tenuis-acropora-coral ; [reeflex] https://se.reeflex.net/tiere/602_Acropora_tenuis.htm ; [PMC7020822] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7020822/ ; [Curacao 2009] https://www.ingentaconnect.com/contentone/umrsmas/bullmar/2009/00000084/00000003/art00003 ; [AEFW Springer 2013] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs00338-013-1101-6 ; [AEFW reefs.com] https://reefs.com/pest/acropra-eating-flatworm-aefw/

## 12. Pulsing xenia - Xenia / Heteroxenia

- Temperature: not sourced in a number. Estimate: 75-80 F (24-27 C).
- PAR: not sourced. One source notes lower light slows pulsing [search summary]. Estimate: 75-200 PAR.
- Flow: moderate, indirect. Too much flow can stop pulsing (the water movement does the work) [search summary, forum].
- Alkalinity: one guide says above 8 dKH encourages pulsing; below 7 dKH often correlates with reduced pulsing. A forum post reported pulsing stopping below a unit it did not define. Treat these as anecdotal.
- Iodine: one hobbyist attributes reduced pulsing to iodine depletion as colonies grow; dosing iodine restored it (anecdote).
- Calcium, magnesium, nutrients: not sourced.
- Growth: fast, colony-forming by stolons, spreads across the rock. Not sourced in a number.
- Feeding: a 2023 study shows feeding substantially affects Xenia umbellata holobiont physiology and pulsation rate, which is lower without food (Hill et al., Ecology and Evolution 2023) [search summary].
- Pulse rate: no reference range found in a peer-reviewed source. A university study counted pulses over 30 s and doubled the count to give pulses per minute, but the range was not reported in the summary read. Estimate: about 20-60 pulses/min at rest, faster with light and flow. Stops with no identified cause, or with strong flow, or with low alkalinity/iodine (anecdote).
- Chemical warfare: not sourced. Xenia is not known for sweepers.
- Mechanism: a 2025 PNAS paper on Xenia umbellata (Nadir, Lotan, Benayahu, reported by Tel Aviv and Haifa universities) found no central pacemaker. Heteroxenia fuscescens: Kremien et al., PNAS 2013, pulsation thrusts water up and raises net photosynthesis up to an order of magnitude over non-pulsing state in lab [search summary; paper not fetched].
- Sizes sold: frags 1-2 in with 2-4 polyps [retail].
- Prices: not verified. Estimate: $10-$40 frag, varies by morph.
- Colors: purple, green, white, orange. Fluorescence: not sourced.
- Failure modes: stops pulsing; can become a nuisance by overgrowing rock; sensitive to iodine and alk swings.
- Difficulty: beginner-friendly.

## 0. Cross-cutting: bleaching thresholds and disease

Bleaching threshold (field, peer-reviewed/NOAA):
- NOAA Coral Reef Watch defines the bleaching threshold as 1 C above the maximum monthly mean (MMM), traced to Glynn and D'Croz (1990). Degree heating weeks (DHW) accumulate HotSpots at or above that threshold over 12 weeks [NOAA-CRW].
- DHW 4 C-weeks: significant bleaching. DHW 8 C-weeks: widespread bleaching and significant mortality likely [NOAA-CRW].
- A 2024 Coral Reefs paper argues the DHW definition underpredicts bleaching; a 0.4 C cutoff, 11-week window and 3 DHW threshold improved skill by 70% globally [Springer s00338-024-02512-w; search summary].
- Hoegh-Guldberg 1999 (Marine and Freshwater Research 50:839-866) is the classic reference on climate change and bleaching; the 1 C figure itself is not verified as stated in that paper [CSIRO].
- Aquarium translation: bleaching onset temperatures are not published for most species. Estimate: maximum tolerated is about 1 C above the local summer maximum for hobby species, which is roughly 28-29 C for most reef species kept at 24-26 C. Temperatures sustained above 28 C are associated with bubble coral deflation (section 8).

Brown jelly disease (Euphyllia and others):
- The direct cause of brown jelly outbreaks is unknown. The mass is coral tissue, zooxanthellae, bacteria, viruses and ciliates; ciliates are the most visible, and the MacEwan University study suggests they are opportunistic invaders [RB-Brown; MacEwan journal].
- Only one dip, Polyp Lab Reef Primer (potassium salts), visibly affected the ciliates in one hobbyist's microscope tests. The evidence is observational and not peer-reviewed; the author asks for repeat testing [RB-Brown].
- Reef2Reef anecdotes conflict: ciprofloxacin, Chemiclean, lugol/peroxide dips, and removal and isolation. Consensus: isolate, stabilize water, reduce stress from flow and light [Reef2Reef thread: https://www.reef2reef.com/threads/euphyllia-receding.958755/].

Tissue recession (RTN/STN):
- Reef2Reef Euphyllia recession thread points to excess light and flow, and calcium below about 380 ppm. Anecdotal [Reef2Reef].
