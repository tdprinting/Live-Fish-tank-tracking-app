# Firefish goby and blue-green chromis (research 41)

Research update: 2026-10-20. Supports the planned sim species for the firefish goby (*Nemateleotris magnifica*) and the blue-green chromis (*Chromis viridis*). Each note is tagged **Observed** (from a species account, study or retailer page, with a link) or **Design estimate** (a provisional model value that no source gives directly).

Access notes: the FishBase firefish page returned HTTP 503 and the Fishes of Australia chromis page returned HTTP 403 when fetched. Their figures below come from search-result summaries of those pages. LiveAquaria and other store prices are cached listings, not confirmed 2026 prices.

## 1. Firefish goby (*Nemateleotris magnifica*)

### 1.1 Identity and size

- **Taxonomy.** Described by Fowler in 1938. Dartfish (Ptereleotridae in older classifications; some sources place it in Microdesmidae, subfamily Ptereleotrinae). One Wikipedia summary lists Gobiidae, which conflicts with the dartfish placement in the other sources. The sim treats it as a dartfish. [Wikipedia: Fire goby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby); [Seatizens](https://seatizens.sc/species/nemateleotris-magnifica-fowler-1938/)
- **Max length.** 9.0 cm TL for males or unsexed fish (FishBase). Wikipedia also gives 9 cm (3.5 in). **Observed.** [FishBase summary 6629](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/6629); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby)
- **Typical adult in a tank.** Not published. Retailers list the fish as "maximum size 3 inches" (about 7.6 cm). The sim uses 8 cm typical. **Design estimate.**
- **Size sold.** LiveAquaria sells a 2.5 in (about 6.4 cm) WYSIWYG fish. **Observed.** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/firefish-goby-2-5-wysiwyg.oembed)
- **Growth rate.** Not found. **Design estimate:** juveniles 4 cm to adult size in about 9–12 months.
- **Lifespan.** About 3 years, per a retailer care sheet. Wild lifespan is not published. **Observed (retailer).** [Saltwater Aquarium Blog](https://www.saltwateraquariumblog.com/saltwater-firefish-goby-quick-facts-care-guide-diet-and-more/)

### 1.2 Body shape and colour

- **Shape.** Slender and elongate. The sources give no body-depth ratio. **Design estimate:** depth/length about 0.18 (SL basis), slimmer than the diamond goby's sleeper body.
- **Fins.** The dorsal fins are "very long" (Wikipedia). The first dorsal spine is elongated and is flicked back and forth as a signal to other fish of its kind. One account suggests the spine can lock the fish into a crevice, as in triggerfish. This is reported, not confirmed. **Observed (flicking); unconfirmed (locking).** [Wikipedia: Fire goby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby); [Seatizens](https://seatizens.sc/species/nemateleotris-magnifica-fowler-1938/)
- **Colour.** Bright yellow head, merging into a white body and shading to a red-orange tail. The fish is red overall in the tank trade (the "red firefish" name is a colour morph). **Observed.** Approximate hex: head #F2C12E; body #F4F1EA; tail #D9462A. The hex values are design estimates from the descriptions above.
- **Night colour and stress.** Not found. Retailers say colour fades when the fish is poorly fed, and an under-fed firefish looks dull. **Observed (retailer).** [Quality Marine](https://www.qualitymarine.com/quality-marine/fish/gobies/nemateleotris/firefish-18519/)

### 1.3 Swimming and hover

- **Style.** Pectoral-fin-powered (labriform) hovering just above the bottom. The sources say it sits "just above the bottom, facing into the current". The word "hover" is not used in the Wikipedia text. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby)
- **Hover height.** Not published. **Design estimate:** 1–4 cm above the sand or rock in a burrow-based habitat, with most time within 2 cm of the burrow mouth. Earlier notes listing 0.3–1 m for this species are not supported by any source found. Do not use them.
- **Burst speed.** Not published. **Design estimate:** cruise 0.3 BL/s, burst 3 BL/s (dartfish retreat in a short, fast dart).

### 1.4 Habitat and burrow

- **Depth.** 6–70 m (Wikipedia); FishBase lists 0–70 m, usually 6–28 m. **Observed.** [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/6629)
- **Burrow.** Adults live alone or in pairs in sandy burrows. Juveniles live in small groups. When threatened the fish retreats into a burrow. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby)
- **Distance from burrow.** Sources say it "does not stray far from its burrow". No distance is given. **Design estimate:** feeds within 25 cm of the burrow.
- **Burrow digging.** Not found. Retailers say it uses existing crevices and rock caves. The sim does not dig.

### 1.5 Social

- **Pairs.** Monogamous. Adults live alone or in pairs in burrows. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby)
- **Same-species tank aggression.** Keep one fish per tank or a mated pair. Avoid multiple males, because the dominant pair picks on weaker individuals. **Observed (retailer, multiple).** [Saltwater Aquarium Blog](https://www.saltwateraquariumblog.com/saltwater-firefish-goby-quick-facts-care-guide-diet-and-more/); [Quality Marine](https://www.qualitymarine.com/quality-marine/fish/gobies/nemateleotris/firefish-18519/)
- **Spacing.** Not measured. **Design estimate:** a second firefish must stay more than 40 cm from the first, or it is chased.
- **Signalling.** The dorsal flick is a signal to conspecifics. **Observed.**

### 1.6 Diet and feeding

- **Wild.** Waits near the bottom, facing the current, for small invertebrates; zooplankton and crustacean larvae are the main items. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby)
- **Captive.** Brine shrimp, mysis, and zooplankton. Feed twice a day. Fish that are under-fed lose colour. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_goby); [Quality Marine](https://www.qualitymarine.com/quality-marine/fish/gobies/nemateleotris/firefish-18519/)
- **Feeding position.** Takes food from the water column above the burrow, facing into the current. **Observed.**
- **Sim diet tags:** MYSIS, BRINE, ZOOPLANKTON, PODS.

### 1.7 Daily activity and night

- **Diurnal.** Active by day. Retreats into a burrow or crevice at night and when startled. **Observed (retailer and Wikipedia).**
- **Sleep.** Retires to a hole at night. **Observed (general).**

### 1.8 Jumping

**Observed (multiple).** Firefish are jumpers. Sources agree that a stressed firefish will try to leap out of an open tank. One care sheet notes a gap of about 2 cm at the back of the tank, near filter pipes, is enough for a spooked fish to launch out. Kraken Corals suggests a Plexiglas edge at least 25 cm (10 in) tall on open-top halide tanks. [Kraken Corals](https://krakencorals.co.uk/products/red-firefish); [Wikipedia: Elegant firefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elegant_firefish) (the related species is described as a prolific leaper).

- **What triggers it.** Startle is the common trigger: a threatening tankmate, a newly added fish, or sudden disturbance. A lights-on or lights-off trigger is plausible but not documented. **Observed (forum reports).** [Nano-Reef forum](https://www.nano-reef.com/forums/topic/36687-firefish-jump/); [ReefCentral archive](https://archive.reefcentral.com/forums/showthread.php?p=11493565)
- **Rate.** No data found. Some fish dive for cover instead of leaping. **Design estimate:** on an open-top tank, 6% chance per startle event that the fish jumps. With a tight lid, 0%. Startle events from tankmates are rare in the sim, so expect a jump about once every few weeks in an open-top tank with bullies.

### 1.9 Threats

- **Hawkfish.** No source names firefish as a falco hawkfish target. Falco hawkfish are semi-aggressive and harass similarly shaped bottom fish. Sources advise against housing them with smaller gobies, dartfish or other passive fish unless those are added first. **Design estimate:** a 4–5 cm firefish is at real risk from a falco hawkfish that is already established. Set a moderate threat weight.
- **Dottybacks.** Sources list dottybacks as a poor match for firefish. **Observed (retailer).** [Saltwater Aquarium Blog](https://www.saltwateraquariumblog.com/saltwater-firefish-goby-quick-facts-care-guide-diet-and-more/)
- **Shy fish.** Retailers describe firefish as shy. They can be injured or starve when housed with aggressive or semi-aggressive tankmates. **Observed (retailer).**

### 1.10 Reproduction and life stages

- **Sexual system.** Not stated in the sources. The pair is monogamous. **Observed (pair bonding only).**
- **Spawning in the burrow.** Not documented in any source found. Do not model it.
- **Juveniles.** Juveniles form small groups; adults pair or live alone. **Observed (Wikipedia).**
- **Pelagic larval duration.** Not found. Not modelled.

### 1.11 Water parameters and hardiness

- **Temperature.** 72–78 °F (22.2–25.6 °C) per retailer. Some sources give 23–27 °C (73–81 °F). **Observed (retailer, two sources).**
- **Salinity.** SG 1.020–1.025. **Observed (retailer).**
- **pH.** 8.1–8.4. **Observed (retailer).**
- **dKH.** 8–12. **Observed (retailer).**
- **Nitrate.** No firm number found. **Design estimate:** keep under 10 ppm.
- **Shipping and disease.** Not found in the sources for firefish. Retailers describe it as hardy once established, and the main risk is jumping. **Design estimate:** shipping loss 10%, higher than the diamond goby.

### 1.12 Care

- **Minimum tank.** 20 gal per most retailers (LiveAquaria 20 gal; Kraken Corals 10 gal or larger; one source 29 gal). The sim uses 20 gal (76 L) as the minimum. **Observed.**
- **Care level.** Easy (LiveAquaria). **Observed.**
- **Reef-safe.** Yes. **Observed.**
- **Store price (US, cached listings).** LiveAquaria $34.99 (sold out in the cached page), $29.99 (second listing), $44.99 (2.5 in WYSIWYG, in stock); other roundups $34.99–$49.99; Fishyhub $49.99. **Observed (cached, not 2026 verified).** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/firefish); [Fishyhub](https://fishyhub.com/us/product-detail/firefish-goby-32382)
- **Sim price.** $39.99 (per fish, typical size), **design estimate** from the listings above.

## 2. Blue-green chromis (*Chromis viridis*)

### 2.1 Identity and size

- **Name.** Also called the blue-green puller, green chromis, or blue-green damselfish. Some sources treat *Chromis caerulea* (blue puller) as the same species. *Chromis atripectoralis* (black-axil puller) is larger, at 12 cm, and is often confused with *C. viridis*. **Observed.** [Wikipedia: Chromis viridis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_viridis); [Wikipedia: Chromis atripectoralis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_atripectoralis)
- **Max length.** 10 cm TL (FishBase). Wikipedia also gives 10 cm (3.9 in). **Observed.** [FishBase summary 5679](https://fishbase.org/summary/5679)
- **Typical adult in a tank.** 7–8 cm. **Design estimate.**
- **Size sold.** LiveAquaria sells 2–2.75 in (about 5–7 cm) fish. Another listing shows 3/4–1.5 in (about 2–4 cm) juveniles. **Observed.** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/blue-green-reef-chromis-3-lot-2-to-2-75-wysiwyg)
- **Growth rate.** Not found. **Design estimate:** 3 cm to 7 cm in about 8–10 months.
- **Lifespan.** 8–15 years in a well-kept aquarium (LiveAquaria). **Observed (retailer).** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/product/115/)

### 2.2 Body shape and colour

- **Shape.** Deep-bodied damselfish with a forked tail. FishBase gives body depth as 2.0–2.1 times in SL, so depth/length is about 0.48 (SL basis). **Observed (FishBase), converted to depth ratio by the author.**
- **Fin counts.** Dorsal VII, 9–10; anal II, 10–11; pectoral 16–18; gill rakers 28–33. The pectoral fin has no black spot at its axil, which separates it from the black-axil puller. **Observed.** [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329)
- **Colour.** An iridescent bluish-green damselfish. Pale apple-green and light blue in the Wikipedia description. Approximate hex: body #46B3A4; highlight #B9E7E3; fins translucent. **Observed (descriptions); hex values are design estimates.**
- **Breeding colour.** Males turn more yellowish during breeding, and some turn intense yellow to orange while spawning. Males turn pale yellow when preparing a nest. **Observed.** [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_viridis)
- **Night colour.** No source reports a night or dusk colour change. **Not found.** Do not model it.
- **Stress colour.** Not found.

### 2.3 Swimming

- **Style.** Caudal-fin-powered schooling, mid-water. The school hovers above branching *Acropora* heads. **Observed.** [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329)
- **Cruise and burst speeds.** Not published. The bioRxiv / *Frontiers in Fish Science* escape paper on *C. viridis* (schools of 4, 8 and 16 at standard length 2.9–3.7 cm) gives turning-rate and distance differences but no body-lengths-per-second values. **Observed (no value).** [Frontiers in Fish Science 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/fish-science/articles/10.3389/frish.2023.1294259/full)
- **Escape kinematics (relative).** Turning rate about 25% lower in groups of 16 than in groups of 4. Distance covered in the first 42 ms about 35% lower in groups of 16. **Observed (relative).**
- **Design estimate:** cruise 1.2 BL/s, burst 6 BL/s, with the escape turn at about 25% lower rate in large schools (the observed relative effect).

### 2.4 Habitat and zone in the water column

- **Depth.** 1–12 m (FishBase). **Observed.** [FishBase summary 5679](https://fishbase.org/summary/5679)
- **Zone.** Mid-water over branching corals, especially *Acropora*. Schools in sheltered lagoons and coastal reefs. **Observed.** [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329)
- **Shelter.** Retreats into coral branches when threatened and at night, sleeping among the branches. **Observed.** [Nausicaa](https://www.nausicaa.fr/en/my-visit/animals/blue-green-damselfish)

### 2.5 Social

- **Wild group.** Large schools above branching corals. Group size is not given as a number. **Observed (qualitative).**
- **Captive group size.** Sources recommend at least 5–6 fish, ideally an odd number (5, 7 or 9), to spread aggression. Groups of 3–4 usually produce one dominant survivor. **Observed (multiple hobby sources); anecdotal.** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/blue-green-reef-chromis-3-lot-2-to-2-75-wysiwyg); [Top Shelf Aquatics](https://topshelfaquatics.com/blogs/news/why-chromis-dont-actually-school-long-term)
- **Hierarchy.** Dominance hierarchies form within days of introduction. Open aggression can show at 6–12 months, with subordinates pushed behind rockwork and losing condition. Adding fish later to an established group usually causes fast deaths of the newcomers. **Observed (hobby, anecdotal).**
- **Last-one-standing pattern.** In 3–5 fish groups, one dominant individual bullies the others within about three months. The survivor patrols alone. **Observed (hobby, anecdotal).** [Top Shelf Aquatics](https://topshelfaquatics.com/blogs/news/why-chromis-dont-actually-school-long-term)
- **Sim rule.** Groups of 3–4 lose one fish to bullying within 90 days (design estimate, p=0.5 per fish in that window). Groups of 6+ lose none in 90 days.
- **Aggression to other species.** Chromis are peaceful toward non-chromis fish, but larger predatory fish eat them. Sources say not to house them with groupers, lionfish or eels. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_viridis)

### 2.6 Diet and feeding

- **Wild.** Zooplankton in the water column; phytoplankton, algae, copepods and amphipods; unhatched eggs. Ram-jaws zooplankton. **Observed.** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_viridis); [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329)
- **Captive.** Several feedings a day with a varied diet: meaty foods, herbivore flakes and frozen preparations. **Observed.** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/product/115/)
- **Sim diet tags:** ZOOPLANKTON, PLANKTON, CYCLOPS, PODS, MYSIS, BRINE, FLAKE.

### 2.7 Daily activity and night

- **Diurnal.** Active in the day, in mid-water schools. **Observed.**
- **Sleep.** Sleeps among coral branches at night, and moves its fins about twice as much asleep as awake (one source). **Observed (one source).** [Nausicaa](https://www.nausicaa.fr/en/my-visit/animals/blue-green-damselfish)

### 2.8 Jumping

- **Observed.** No source reports chromis jumping. The school is mid-water and calm. Retailers recommend a lid for all fish, but do not single out chromis.
- **Design estimate:** 1% chance per startle event that a chromis jumps, with a tight lid at 0. The sim treats chromis as a low jump risk.

### 2.9 Predators and threats in a reef tank

- **Hawkfish.** Falco hawkfish (*Cirrhitichthys falco*) are semi-aggressive. Sources say it can threaten small fish and shrimp, and it harasses similar bottom-living fish. No source names chromis as a target. **Observed (genus), chromis risk not documented.** [Practical Fishkeeping](https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/care-guide-for-the-falco-or-dwarf-hawkfish/); [Top Shelf Aquatics](https://topshelfaquatics.com/pages/saltwater-fish/falco-hawkfish)
- **Dottybacks.** Poor match, per retailers. **Observed (retailer).**
- **Design estimate:** chromis take low harassment from hawkfish because they are mid-water and shoal. A lone chromis is at higher risk than a school.

### 2.10 Reproduction and life stages

- **Sexual system.** Males build nests on sand or rubble, and several females lay eggs in one male's nest. The male guards and fans the eggs and eats unhatched eggs. This is a polygynous nesting system (exact sexual system not stated in the sources). **Observed.** [Fishes of Australia 329](https://fishesofaustralia.net.au/home/species/329); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromis_viridis)
- **Spawning colour.** Males turn yellowish or intense yellow to orange. **Observed.**
- **Egg hatching.** Eggs hatch in 2–3 days. **Observed (Wikipedia).**
- **Pelagic larval duration.** Not found. Not modelled.
- **Juveniles.** Small and pale. Juveniles can be kept as 3/4–1.5 in fish in the trade. **Observed (retailer size).**
- **Aquarium breeding.** Some hobbyists breed the fish at home. **Observed (Wikipedia, anecdotal).**

### 2.11 Water parameters and hardiness

- **Temperature.** 72–78 °F (22.2–25.6 °C). **Observed (LiveAquaria).**
- **Salinity.** SG 1.020–1.025. **Observed (LiveAquaria).**
- **pH.** 8.1–8.4. **Observed (LiveAquaria).**
- **dKH.** 8–12. **Observed (LiveAquaria).**
- **Hardiness.** Hardy and peaceful once settled. **Observed (retailer).**
- **Shipping losses.** Forum reports describe high mortality in bulk shipping. Chromis ship poorly. **Observed (forum, anecdotal).** [Reef2Reef](https://www.reef2reef.com/threads/dr-reefs-quarantined-chromis.1079543/)
- **Disease.** Chromis have a higher-than-average risk of uronema (marine velvet). One vendor quarantines 28 days with a protocol aimed at uronema. The effectiveness of quarantine for uronema is debated. Ich and velvet can appear to clear and return. **Observed (forum, vendor claim); anecdotal.** [Reef2Reef](https://www.reef2reef.com/threads/dr-reefs-quarantined-chromis.1079543/)
- **Sim rule.** Shipping loss 15% (design estimate); uronema risk on introduction, 5% per fish in a tank without quarantine (design estimate).

### 2.12 Care

- **Minimum tank.** 30 gal (LiveAquaria; Quality Marine 30 gal or larger). A school of six needs 55 gal or more. A school of five needs a 75 gal or larger tank (Aquatic Unlimited). The sim uses 110 L (29 gal) as the minimum for a small group, and 225 L (60 gal) for a group of six. **Observed (retailer and Wikipedia).**
- **Care level.** Easy. **Observed.**
- **Reef-safe.** Yes. **Observed.**
- **Store price (US, cached listings).** LiveAquaria $9.59 (sold out), 3-lot 2–2.75 in $49.99 (sold out), a separate product page shows $24.99 in stock at 3/4–1.5 in. Aquatic Unlimited $19.99–$59.99. **Observed (cached, not 2026 verified).** [LiveAquaria](https://www.liveaquaria.com/product/115/); [Aquatic Unlimited](https://aquaticsunlimited.com/?p=12032)
- **Sim price.** $19.99 per fish, typical juvenile size. **Design estimate** from the listings above.
