### 1. Peppermint angelfish (Centropyge boylei, also Paracentropyge boylei)
- **Price:** Historic reports US$19,995 asking for a juvenile (NYAquatic, 2013, Reef Builders); "$25,000-$30,000" rumoured price (Blue Harbor report, AquaNerd); offers up to $30,000 for the Waikiki Aquarium specimen (Practical Fishkeeping; Wikipedia). No 2024-2026 retail price found [?]. Sim range: US$20,000-30,000 [I, from the above].
- **Rarity:** Only recorded from Rarotonga, Cook Islands; lives 55-120 m (Wikipedia), "never observed shallower than 53 m" (search snippet); collection needs a technical-diving rebreather. Described 1992 (Randall and Pyle), type specimen taken at 80-100 m.
- **Size:** max 7 cm TL (Wikipedia). Min tank [I]: 450 L (about 120 gal); sources only say "large, dim aquarium with ledges and caves", one keeper used a 130 gal with a chiller.
- **Reef-safe:** Dwarf angel; most Centropyge are reef-safe with caution, may nip LPS/clam mantles [I]. [?] no source on this species.
- **Water:** Deepwater, so cool. Waikiki Aquarium specimen kept in ~21 C (70 F) water (search snippet); a chiller was used by one keeper. Sim: 21-24 C [I], SG 1.025 [I], low light.
- **Diet:** "Finicky eater, varied diet" (snippet, genus note). Sim [I]: mysis, enriched brine, fine marine algae/sponge-based prepared food, 2-3 small feedings/day.
- **Behaviour:** Lives on coral rubble, overhangs and caves on steep outer slopes (Wikipedia). Dwarf angel: shy, hugs rockwork, short darting swims [I]. Territorial toward other Centropyge [I, genus norm]. Retreats to caves at night [I].
- **Appearance:** Deep oval body (Wikipedia). Ground colour red-orange, about #E8452B [I]; five white vertical bands (Wikipedia: "five white vertical bands on a reddish-orange background"); face and chin white; dorsal and anal fins have white margins; tail translucent. Juvenile vs adult: [?].

### 2. Gem tang (Zebrasoma gemmatum)
- **Price:** about US$746.99 LiveAquaria; $999.99 for a small fish at Top Shelf Aquatics (search-snippet listings, undated, roughly 2024-2026); guides quote $800-$1,500, up to $3,000-6,000 for large. Sim range: US$750-1,500 [I].
- **Rarity:** Endemic to the SW Indian Ocean (South Africa, Mozambique, Madagascar, Mauritius, Reunion); 25-60 m depth, low collection volume (Wikipedia, snippet).
- **Size:** max 22 cm TL (Wikipedia). Sellers: 8-9 in (~20-23 cm). Min tank: 125 to 240 gal recommended (snippet); sim min 570 L (150 gal) [I].
- **Reef-safe:** Yes (snippet). Herbivore, may graze some macroalgae [I].
- **Water:** 22-26 C (72-78 F), SG 1.020-1.025, pH 8.1-8.4 (snippet). Deeper species so keep towards cool end [I].
- **Diet:** Herbivore: nori, spirulina, herbivore pellets, occasional mysis (snippet). Sim: algae/nori daily, grazing all day [I].
- **Behaviour:** Rare, territorial, usually solitary; semi-aggressive; poorly tolerates other Zebrasoma and similar-shaped tangs (Wikipedia; snippet). Add last. Needs open swimming room and hiding spots (snippet). Active cruiser [I]. Wild lifespan up to 25 yr, captive 7-10 yr (snippet). Tangs wedge into rock at night and darken [I, genus norm].
- **Appearance:** Deep body (depth 1.6-1.9 in SL, Wikipedia); dark brown, about #3B2A20 [I], covered with many small white spots (round, some elongated) over head, body, dorsal and anal fins (Wikipedia); caudal fin yellow, about #F2C230 [I]. Typical Zebrasoma white tail spine, protruding snout [I]. Juvenile: [?].

### 3. Wrought iron butterflyfish (Chaetodon daedalma)
- **Price:** $1,800 + shipping for LiveAquaria's earlier fish, sold within minutes (Reef Builders, 2009); a pair "$5,000 or more" (Reef Builders, 2010); Reef Exclusive original price $4,440 (sold out, current-era listing); wholesale $1,200 quoted 2010 on forum. Sim range: US$1,800-4,500 [I].
- **Rarity:** Endemic to Japan (south of Sagami Bay, Ryukyu, Izu, Ogasawara); deeper than 10 m on clear outer reefs; export from Japan limited.
- **Size:** ~15 cm TL (Wikipedia). Min tank [I]: 570-750 L.
- **Reef-safe:** Unknown/risky; grazes fanworms, hydroids, tunicates, barnacles, gastropods (Wikipedia); likely to eat tubeworms and may pick at soft corals/zoas [I].
- **Water:** Warm-temperate Japan: 20-25 C [I]; SG 1.025 [I].
- **Diet:** Algae plus small benthic invertebrates (Wikipedia). Captive [I]: mysis, finely chopped shellfish, enriched brine, marine algae sheets, multiple small feedings daily. "Vigorous appetite" noted for the LiveAquaria fish.
- **Behaviour:** Sociable, nomadic; schools of 10+ ranging widely, sometimes massing in a tight "wrought-iron ball"; pairs breed (Wikipedia). Reef Builders 2010 titled "beautiful pair... caught". Inquisitive in a tank (Reef Builders 2009). Sim [I]: pair-bonded, tolerant of conspecifics, swims in open water, rests near rock at night.
- **Appearance:** Mostly metallic black, about #1B1B1E [I]; each scale has pale-yellow centre with broad black border giving a net/wrought-iron lattice pattern; yellow edges on dorsal, anal and caudal fins (Wikipedia), about #E8C63A [I]. Disc-shaped butterfly profile [I]. Juvenile: [?].

### 4. Bandit angelfish (Apolemichthys arcuatus, banded angelfish)
- **Price:** Wild adults historically $600-$800 US (AquaNerd; forum vendor posts $600-700; AquaTouch $750 in 2010). Tiny juvenile "well over $3000" (Reef Builders 2011). Tank-bred 1.3 in juvenile from Kamihata: Unique Fins "considering offers over $5k" (Reef Builders, 10 Sep 2024). Sim range: US$800 wild-equivalent to $5,000+ tank-bred [I].
- **Rarity:** Endemic to Hawaii and Johnston Atoll; wild supply ended when West Hawaii aquarium fishery closed in 2021 (Reef Builders 2024); 10-183 m (Wikipedia), juveniles deeper than adults.
- **Size:** 18 cm TL (Wikipedia). Min tank [I]: 570 L (150 gal).
- **Reef-safe:** Unknown; diet is sponges so not a coral eater by diet, but angelfish of this genus may nip polyps/clam mantles [I].
- **Water:** Deep Hawaiian reef, 22-25 C [I]; SG 1.025 [I].
- **Diet:** Mostly sponges, plus algae, hydroids, eggs (Wikipedia). Captive: wild fish sometimes eat only mysis, others mysis and pellets (vendor posts); the 2024 tank-bred fish accepts dry foods. Sim [I]: sponge-based angel formulas, mysis, 3x/day.
- **Behaviour:** Rocky reefs, under overhangs and in caves (Wikipedia). Territorial [I, genus norm], shy initially; sim: cave-dwelling, hovers near ledges.
- **Appearance:** Pale white to cream body, about #EDE9DD [I], with one broad black band starting just above the mouth, edged white on top, running across the head and back to the end of the dorsal fin; a matching white-edged black band along the caudal and anal fins (Wikipedia). Reef Builders: "striking black and white". Juvenile: [?] (tiny fish are sold at about 1 in).

### 5. Clarion angelfish (Holacanthus clarionensis)
- **Price:** Captive-bred: first sold ~US$5,000 (LiveAquaria Diver's Den, 2013); two fish listed $7,499 each Sept 2018 (Reef Builders; price up because of CITES paperwork). Quality Marine expected ~$3,000 in 2013 (AquaNerd). Current 2024-2026 price [?]. Sim range: US$5,000-7,500 [I].
- **Rarity:** Eastern Pacific, Revillagigedo/Clarion area off Mexico; wild collection prohibited since protected area created; IUCN Vulnerable, CITES Appendix II (Wikipedia; Reef Builders). Captive-bred supply from Bali Aquarich.
- **Size:** 20 cm (Wikipedia), 23 cm / 10 in (Reef Builders). Min tank [I]: 1000 L (264 gal) for adult.
- **Reef-safe:** Generally not; large Holacanthus angelfish eat sessile inverts (clam mantles, soft/LPS) [I].
- **Water:** Rocky reefs to 30 m, tropical eastern Pacific, 22-26 C [I]. Called "bulletproof" by Reef Builders.
- **Diet:** Not stated in sources. Omnivore [I]: sponge-based foods, mysis, algae.
- **Behaviour:** Solitary or in shoals; poorly studied (Wikipedia). Cleaned by other fish in the wild (video listing). Sim [I]: bold, territorial, congener-aggressive.
- **Appearance:** Juvenile: yellowish-orange body and fins with blue vertical bars, blue lips and fin margins; bars fade with maturity. Adult: brownish face, reddish-orange body behind head, browner rear body, bright orange dorsal and anal fins, vermilion caudal fin (Wikipedia). Hex [I]: body #D8672A, fins #F2762E, tail #D8421F, face #6B4A33.

### 6. Gold torch (Euphyllia glabrescens, gold morph)
- **Price:** Aquarium Depot (Canada, CAD listings) $249.99 Gold Indo Torch, $299.99 Gold Blue Tip, $349.99 Solid Gold Blue Tip per single head; Reef Chasers "Gringott's Gold Torch" $125 (single head, aquacultured); Reef Builders 2019: Australian gold torch single polyp once $60-80 rose to $200+; Fishkeeping News 2019: about GBP 250/head UK. Sim range: US$125-350 per head [I].
- **Rarity:** Morph is rare because of the colour; species common and exported from Indonesia/Fiji under quota (Wikipedia: 2005 quotas 28,000 and 6,000 pieces).
- **Size/stinging:** Corallite 20-30 mm wide, phaceloid colony, thin sharp-edged walls; long sweeper tentacles with potent nematocysts (Wikipedia). Sweeper reach about 10-15 cm [I]. Stings neighbouring corals.
- **Habitat:** 1-35 m (Wikipedia). Light: moderate PAR 100-150 [I]; flow: low-moderate [I]; growth: moderate, adds heads over months [I]. Gensou care guide exists (search result) but was not fetched.
- **Feeding:** Photosynthetic plus target-fed mysis/meaty foods weekly [I].
- **Appearance:** Wikipedia: often bicoloured, green with yellow tips / beige with green tips. Gold morph: golden-yellow tentacles, about #D9A81E [I], often with blue/green tips (e.g. "Gold Blue Tip" listing name), brown-gold base; tentacles are tubular with knob-like tips (Wikipedia).

### 7. Bounce mushroom (Rhodactis inchoata 'bounce')
- **Price:** Record: one 3-inch polyp sold $6,000 at Reefapalooza, bought two weeks earlier for $3,000 (Reef Builders, 13 Oct 2015). Typical "common bounce" $100-300 per polyp (ReefCentral thread, undated). Standard Rhodactis $40-80 per rock/polyp (Reef Builders 2016). Sim range: US$150-300 common, $1,000-6,000 premium [I].
- **Rarity:** Cannot tell which Rhodactis will "bounce" (grow vesicles) -- unpredictable. Original strain from World Wide Corals. Slow growth (Reef Builders).
- **Light/flow:** low-moderate light, low flow [I, typical corallimorph]. Reef Builders 2023: a UCA strain only "bounces" under blue-rich LED.
- **Behaviour:** Corallimorph, no skeleton (Wikipedia, Rhodactis); can sting neighbours slightly [I]; spreads slowly [I].
- **Appearance:** Brown/olive mat disc; surface covered in "comically enlarged" fluorescent vesicles, neon orange with green highlights, like "a ring of neon marbles" (Reef Builders 2015); record polyp more than 3 in across. Hex [I]: disc #5A4A30, vesicles #FF7A1A with #6CFF4A highlights. Other bounce colours exist (Reef Builders: "rainbow colors").

### 8. Scolymia (Homophyllia australis)
- **Price:** LiveAquaria: Red $149.99 (sold out), Warpaint $297.99 (sold out), Bleeding Sour Apple $348.99; Top Shelf Aquatics UFO Scolymia $449.99. Reef Chasers: wholesale starts around $150. Sim range: US$150-450 [I].
- **Rarity:** Single solitary polyp, hard to aquaculture; wild Australian/Indo-Pacific exports (Reef Chasers).
- **Light/flow:** Top Shelf: PAR about 50-150; Reef Chasers: up to ~200 PAR, too much light more harmful; Reef Builders: moderate-to-low light, moderate indirect flow. Place low on the sandbed with space around it, sting reach about 5-8 cm [I].
- **Parameters:** 76-78 F (24-26 C), SG 1.025-1.026, dKH 8-12, Ca 400-500, Mg 1250-1350 (Reef Builders 2024).
- **Feeding:** Spot-feed meaty foods 2-3x/week (brine, mysis); switch off flow while feeding; remove leftovers.
- **Growth:** Slow [I]. Fish that dump sand on it are a problem (Reef Builders).
- **Appearance:** Single large fleshy cup-shaped polyp, rounded to oval, with raised radial septa ridges; colours green, orange, red, pink, purple, blue, yellow; named morphs Master Scolys, Bleeding Apples, Superman, War Paints, UFOs (Reef Builders). Typical pattern: contrasting centre and rim (e.g. red/green "bleeding apple") [I].

### 11. Peacock mantis shrimp (Odontodactylus scyllarus)
- **Price:** Reef Exclusive $130 (sold out); That Fish Place medium $89.99 (in-store); guides $20-$500+. Sim range: US$90-250 [I].
- **Size:** 3-18 cm (Wikipedia). Min tank [I]: 150 L, 2x 30 cm sand depth, glass-safe acrylic recommended.
- **Reef-safe:** No. Eats fish, snails, crabs, clams, scallops; burrows into live rock; may damage coral; can crack glass (Wikipedia).
- **Water:** Tropical Indo-Pacific 24-27 C [I], SG 1.024-1.026 [I].
- **Diet:** Shell-crushing smasher; snails, crabs, bivalves (Wikipedia). Captive: live/frozen crab, clams, shrimp, 2-3x/week [I].
- **Strike:** peak speed 23 m/s (UC Berkeley 2004; Duke lab 12-23 m/s); over 80 km/h (Wikipedia); impact force 400-1501 N plus cavitation up to 504 N (Duke/ScienceDaily). Spring-loaded saddle-shaped latch (Patek et al., Nature 2004, doi 10.1038/428819a).
- **Behaviour:** Digs U-shaped burrows in loose sediment near reefs at 3-40 m (Wikipedia); solitary, highly territorial [I]; 12 photoreceptor types, polarized light vision.
- **Appearance:** Mostly green carapace and body, about #2FA84F [I], orange legs and swimmerets, about #F28A1E [I], leopard-like spots on the front shell (Wikipedia); smasher clubs (red/orange), blue-edged tail [I]. Eyes on mobile stalks.
- **Sources:**

  - https://doi.org/10.1038/428819a
  - https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/media/releases/2004/04/21_shrimp.shtml
  - https://reefexclusive.com/products/peacock-mantis-shrimp (snippet)

### 12. Pom pom / boxer crab (Lybia tessellata)
- **Price:** about $21.99-$39.99 across Top Shelf, LiveAquaria, WWC, Reef Exclusive, Aquatics Unlimited listings. Sim range: US$25-40 [I]. Not rare-expensive.
- **Size:** about 2.5 cm across (Wikipedia). Min tank 38 L (10 gal) (seller).
- **Reef-safe:** With caution; the carried anemones can sting corals in its path (seller).
- **Water:** 24-26 C (76-78 F), dKH 8-12, pH 8.1-8.4, SG 1.024-1.026 (guide in search snippet).
- **Diet:** brine, mysis, clam, chopped fish; omnivore; uses anemone tentacles to catch food and pass to mouthparts (Wikipedia).
- **Behaviour:** Holds a small anemone (usually Bunodeopsis or Triactis producta) in each claw and waves them as a threat; shy, easier to see at night; hides under ledges; clings to coral with long thin legs; females carry red eggs (Wikipedia; sellers).
- **Appearance:** Trapezoid carapace; dark lines divide it into geometric "stained glass" patches; legs banded and speckled white; slim claws (Wikipedia). Hex [I]: carapace #C9B27A with dark brown #3A2A1E lines, anemones pale pink/white.
- **Sources:**
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lybia_tessellata
  - https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/pom-pom-crab (snippet)
  - https://topshelfaquatics.com/products/pom-pom-crab (snippet)
