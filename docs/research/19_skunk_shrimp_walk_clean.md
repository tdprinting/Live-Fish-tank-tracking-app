# Skunk cleaner shrimp: walking, turning, cleaning, and feeding (animation pass)

Research pass: 2026-10-15. Companion to [12_cleaner_shrimp_detail.md](12_cleaner_shrimp_detail.md) and [16_shrimp_motion_detail.md](16_shrimp_motion_detail.md). Read both first; this note does not repeat their Stenopus, molt, or pleopod material. No YouTube used. Commons not re-fetched this pass.

Tags: **Observed** = stated in a source I read or saw in a search snippet. **Inference** = my extrapolation. **Analogue** = a different decapod or a general caridean pattern, not measured in *Lysmata*.

**Access limits.** The two Journal of Experimental Biology PDFs on crayfish gait (200:1201; 204:471) returned HTTP 403. Several sources below are search snippets, marked "(snippet)". Becker & Grutter 2004 and Chapuis & Bshary 2010 did not appear in search results, so they are not cited. Treat every "Observed" tag from a snippet as provisional until the full text is read.

## Corrections to earlier notes

1. **Pereiopod count.** *Lysmata* has five pereiopod pairs (decapod norm). One search snippet repeated the wrong "10 pairs" figure; file 16 already flags it.
2. **Front-leg rocking.** Caves et al. describe "the white front pair of legs" (PMC6769148). The pereiopod numbers are not stated. File 16 calls it "front pair of legs," which is correct as written.
3. **Upside-down posture is Stenopus, not Lysmata.** The only upside-down observation found is for *Stenopus* under rock undersides, from an aquarium article (anecdotal). Nothing for *Lysmata*.

## 1. Body posture

- **Body height above rock at rest or walking: no source.** **Inference:** body held roughly one leg-length above the substrate on the walking legs, with the body axis near-horizontal and the head slightly raised.
- **Body bob or sway while walking: no source for shrimp.** Crayfish body-height work was not found in readable form. **Analogue:** a crayfish's center of mass shifts forward as the chelae grow; juveniles rely more on posterior legs (PhD summary, snippet). **Inference:** the body should stay mostly level with a small pitch change at each stance. Do not add a vertical bob without a source.
- **Weight-bearing legs:** not directly sourced for *Lysmata*. **Analogue (general caridean pattern):** pereiopods 1–2 chelate; pereiopods 3–5 are the walking legs. **Inference:** P3–P5 carry load; P1–P2 are held forward for food and grooming.
- **Splay:** no data. **Inference:** legs splayed laterally at rest, adducted under the body when moving.
- **Hanging under ledges or on walls, head up or down:** not sourced for *Lysmata*. **Observed (anecdotal, *Stenopus*):** clings to rock undersides upside down (aquarium article, snippet). **Inference:** *Lysmata* can likely hang inverted, but no source.

## 2. Walking gait (decapod analogues)

- **Alternating tetrapod gait, shore crab:** 82% of the time on land, 90% underwater (SICB abstract, snippet; Hui 1992 on *Pachygrapsis crassipes*). **Analogue.**
- **Duty factor, shore crab:** above 50% in air, below 46% submerged. Phase difference between leg rows did not change with speed (Hui 1992, snippet). **Analogue.**
- **Duty factor, rock crab:** shorter contact times and lower duty factor underwater than on land (Martinez, Full & Koehl 2001, snippet). **Analogue.**
- **Metachronal gait, spider crab:** 4-3-2-1 pattern underwater (SICB abstract, snippet). **Analogue.**
- **Crayfish stance:** juveniles of *Procambarus clarkii* show longer stance in posterior legs and shorter in anterior legs (SICB abstract, snippet). **Analogue.**
- **Crayfish swing and stance:** leg extends during swing and flexes during stance; distal joint moves between proximal joint movements (JEB 1997, snippet). **Analogue.**
- **Caridean duty factor, step frequency, stride length vs body length: no data found.** Palaemon, Crangon, and Lysmata walking studies did not appear. **Inference:** duty factor above 50%, short stride of about 0.5–1 body length, 2–4 steps per second at slow walking. Mark as placeholder.
- **Gait pattern for *Lysmata*: no source.** **Inference:** alternating tripod-like (P1/P3/P5 left and right alternate with P2/P4), with frequent pauses. The hermit crab snippet from file 16 supports a tripod-like pattern.

## 3. Turning and sideways walking

- **Pivot in place:** no data for shrimp. **Analogue (crayfish):** curve walking changes step amplitude and stance geometry on the outer legs (*Astacus*, *Procambarus*; snippet). **Inference:** turn by lengthening outer-leg steps and shortening inner-leg steps, with a body yaw of roughly 10–20° per step. Mark as placeholder.
- **Defense turns:** no discernible contralateral leg pattern in crayfish (snippet). **Analogue.** Escape turns should not share the walking gait.
- **Sideways walking:** no data. **Inference:** possible in short bursts, but not sourced for *Lysmata*.
- **Turn speed:** not found. Use placeholder 60–120°/s for slow turns.

## 4. Cleaning mechanics

- **Which appendages touch the client.** Caves et al. define "physical contact" as cleaner-client contact, distinct from "tapping of the antennae on the client's body." The paper does not say which appendages do the contact. **Inference:** P1 forceps pick, P2 long chelae hold or grasp, 3rd maxillipeds pass food and debris to the mouth.
- **Picking motion (alternating left/right, frequency):** no source.
- **Leg rocking (signal):** "rapid back and forth rocking of the white front pair of legs," sometimes with antennae lashing (Caves et al. 2019). **Observed.** Rocking occurred in 51% of interactions with predatory clients versus 12% with non-predatory clients, and was followed by cleaning in 45% versus 5% (Caves et al., via file 16). No Hz.
- **Antennae tapping:** tapping on the client without other contact precedes physical cleaning (Caves et al.). **Observed.** No Hz.
- **What is removed:**
  - Ectoparasites: **Observed.** Monogenean on grouper, day and night (Vaughan et al. 2018, via file 12). Monogenean eggs and larvae, 63% of eggs diurnal and 14% nocturnal, 26% of larvae diurnal (Militz & Hutson 2015, snippet).
  - Injured fish: shrimp cleaned injured sea goldies and reduced wound redness (Vaughan et al. 2018, *Marine Biology*, snippet). **Observed.**
  - Dead tissue and scales: hobbyist sources only. **Inference**, not supported by a study.
  - Mucus: no source.
  - Gnathiid isopods: not found in this pass.
- **Where on the client:** Caves et al. do not state. Earlier note: mouth and gills on eel (Commons; file 16). **Observed (eel only).** Flank and fin contact: **Inference.**
- **How it holds the client:** no source. **Inference:** the shrimp stands on its walking legs at the client's surface, with P1/P2 gripping the body. Shrimp hops onto the fish for *L. grabhami* (anecdotal, snippet).
- **Bout duration:** Caves et al. logged until the first cleaning interaction ended, or up to 1 min with no cleaning. No mean duration reported. **Observed (window only).** The Discover snippet says a shrimp can hold one station for extended periods.

## 5. Antennae

- **Resting and walking posture:** no source for *Lysmata*. **Inference:** antennae forward and slightly raised at rest; the long front pair held out to sense ahead while walking.
- **Lashing and tapping:** lashing accompanies leg rocking; tapping precedes cleaning (Caves et al.). **Observed.** No Hz.
- **Antennule flick rate (lobster analogue, conflicting):** 0.5–2 Hz (1997 study, snippet) versus about 3 Hz (quoted review, unconfirmed). **Analogue.**

## 6. Feeding

- **Food grabbed by P1 forceps and passed to the mouth:** the P1 forceps are used for food and defense (search snippet, anecdotal). **Observed (general).** No kinematic data.
- **Mouthpart handling:** no source. **Inference:** 3rd maxillipeds guide food into the mouth.
