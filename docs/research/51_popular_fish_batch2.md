## 1. Mandarin dragonet (Synchiropus splendidus)

**Size, body, fins, mouth**

1. Adult size is about 7 cm (2.8 in), with males slightly larger than females. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
2. Gensou gives 6-8 cm total length. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
3. Body shape is described as "the same body shape as a regular goby," with a goby-like shape and large pelvic fins used to walk along the floor. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
4. Pectoral fins are described as nearly transparent. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
5. Males have a conspicuously elongated first dorsal fin ray, and are typically 15-20% larger than females. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
6. Aquariadise describes the male dorsal fin as "longer, pointed" compared with the female. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
7. Caudal fin shape and mouth position were not found in any fetched source. Open item.
8. Body depth/length ratio was not found. Open item.

**Colour and pattern**

9. Gensou describes the fish as "draped in psychedelic swirls of blue, orange, green and purple." Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
10. Aquariadise lists the striped form as orange and blue wavy horizontal stripes with a green or blue head. Other forms are a red-and-green form and a spotted form (dark blue spots with yellow lines on a pale yellow-green body). Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
11. All forms have red eyes with large black pupils. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
12. Bulk Reef Supply says the blue (sometimes called green) and red colour variants are commonly seen in the trade. Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
13. Males and females are coloured similarly. Males are told apart by the longer, pointed dorsal fin. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
14. Juveniles reportedly show adult colours about three weeks after hatching. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/

**Swimming and behaviour**

15. The fish hops along rubble and live rock while hunting. It is a bottom-dweller that rarely leaves the lower water column except during mating. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
16. It prefers slow-moving water. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
17. Bulk Reef Supply describes it as peaceful but "somewhat timid." It can be bullied by tankmates and is "best kept singly or as a mated pair." Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
18. Thatpetplace says only one should be kept per tank, and that dragonets can be stressed by aggressive or territorial tankmates. Source: https://www.thatpetplace.com/Synchiropus-splendidus-blue-mandarin-dragonet-213765
19. It buries itself in sand, so the sand bed needs to be deep enough. Live rock and coral should provide "a landscape of hiding places." Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
20. Gensou recommends one or two small caves and dense chaetomorpha for security. It does not describe a sleep spot. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
21. A sleep or roosting spot was not found in any source. Open item.

**Feeding and tank requirements**

22. Copepods are the preferred food. Amphipods were not mentioned in the fetched sources. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
23. A single mandarin can consume thousands of copepods daily. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
24. Wild-caught fish often accept only live copepods. Failure to supply enough food "can lead to starvation, even in seemingly healthy tanks." Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
25. Practical Fishkeeping says the fish "usually need significant live copepods" living in the live rock, with no per-day quantity given. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
26. The minimum tank recommended by Practical Fishkeeping is 100x45x45 cm (40x18x18 in), holding about 200 L (44 gal). Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
27. Bulk Reef Supply gives a minimum of 30 gal. Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
28. ThatPetPlace gives a minimum of 55 gal. Source: https://www.thatpetplace.com/Synchiropus-splendidus-blue-mandarin-dragonet-213765
29. Aquariadise gives 30 gal for a single fish and at least 60 gal for a pair. It also recommends at least 75 lb of live rock per fish. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
30. Gensou says the tank should be at least 12 months old, with at least 50 kg of established live rock, in a system of 200 L or more. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
31. Gensou says a single mandarin is safer in a mature tank. Smaller systems need external copepod culture for the fish's whole life. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359 and https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
32. Captive-bred fish from ORA or Biota are reportedly pre-trained on frozen mysis and brine shrimp. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
33. Bulk Reef Supply says captive-bred fish are often trained to eat frozen and prepared foods, but they still hunt copepods between feedings. They are slow eaters. Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
34. Practical Fishkeeping says a shop-held fish may revert to natural feeding in a reef, so weaning onto frozen food is advised. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
35. Practical Fishkeeping says the fish can take almost a year to reach saleable size. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
36. Pod-population check: a nighttime check with a dimmed torch, looking for dozens to hundreds of tiny specks (search snippet). Source: https://topshelfaquatics.com/blogs/news/mandarin-and-dragonet-care-guide-pods-feeding-and-mature-tank-requirements (page blocked; snippet only)
37. A dragonet can wipe out a tank's copepods in a short time, so the pod population must keep breeding (search snippet). Source: same as 36 (search results)
38. Water: 23-27 C (75-80 F), pH 7.7-8.5, specific gravity 1.020-1.026. Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets

**Breeding**

39. Gensou describes dusk spawning. The male starts displaying its dorsal fin and circling the female 20-40 minutes before lights out. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
40. If the female is receptive, the pair rises together in a slow vertical spiral to within 5-15 cm of the surface, then releases gametes. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
41. Eggs are pelagic, about 0.7 mm in diameter, and hatch in 12-18 hours at 25 C. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
42. Gensou recommends a 60-100 L broodstock tank with abundant live rock. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747
43. Practical Fishkeeping describes a courtship dance and spawning at the surface, with "30-150 eggs" that are buoyant. It does not mention dusk. This conflicts with fact 39. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-do-i-breed-mandarin-dragonets/
44. Gensou advises buying a pair together from a shop that has held them for at least three weeks, because imports often arrive underweight. Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=31747

**Lifespan, care, price, popularity**

45. Captive lifespan is about five years. Wild fish are said to live about 15 years. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
46. Captive-bred mandarins are described as "one of the most iconic saltwater aquarium fish" and "relatively easy to keep" thanks to captive breeding. Source: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets
47. Captive-bred fish are described as "$50-$100 SGD" in Singapore. Live copepod starter cultures are "$15-$30 SGD." Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=30359
48. Aquariadise says captive-bred fish cost up to about $90. Currency and date are not given. Source: https://aquariadise.com/mandarin-goby/
49. ThatPetPlace lists a "Blue Mandarin Dragonet - Small" at $34.99 USD. The page gives a maximum size of 2.5 in, which conflicts with fact 1. Treat the listing as unverified for size. Source: https://www.thatpetplace.com/Synchiropus-splendidus-blue-mandarin-dragonet-213765
50. Cannot confirm LiveAquaria, Bulk Reef Supply or Biota/ORA US retail prices in this batch. Open item.
51. Ich-resistance claim: one guide says mandarins are highly resistant to ich and velvet. It is repeated by only a couple of sources (search snippet). Treat with caution. Source: https://topshelfaquatics.com/blogs/news/mandarin-and-dragonet-care-guide-pods-feeding-and-mature-tank-requirements (snippet only)

## 2. Six line wrasse (Pseudocheilinus hexataenia)

**Size, body, fins, mouth**

52. Adult size is about 3 in (7.6 cm) total length. Sources: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia ; https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
53. Fishtankadvisor says adults reach 3-4 in (7.6-10 cm). Males are the larger sex. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
54. Body shape is described as the "typical wrasse shape (banana with fins)." Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
55. Fin shapes and mouth position were not found in fetched sources. Open item.
56. Body depth/length ratio was not found. Open item.

**Colour and pattern**

57. Body is bluish-purple to lavender, with six bright-orange horizontal stripes along the flanks. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
58. Aquariadise describes the body as blue-to-pink with six contrasting red and orange lines. Source: https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
59. Two of the stripes cut through red eyes. The eyes have white stripes (Reef Builders). Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1 and https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
60. Eyespot: a black eyespot edged in blue sits near the tail, on the dorsal edge of the caudal peduncle. Its purpose is to make predators misjudge the head. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1 and https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
61. Juvenile versus adult colour and male versus female colour differences were not found beyond males being larger. Open item.

**Swimming and behaviour**

62. The fish darts in and around live rock looking for food. Reef Builders calls it "remarkably fast, slinky, and maneuverable." Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
63. Fishtankadvisor says it is active and forages constantly, and is often seen "swimming in schools over the reefs." Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
64. Aggression: Fishtankadvisor calls it "semi-aggressive," says it will bully and harass other wrasses, especially in cramped tanks. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
65. Reef Builders says it is generally peaceful, but it can turn on passive or similar-looking fish, especially newcomers. It has been known to harass fish much larger than itself. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
66. Aquariadise says it can be aggressive toward other fish, especially smaller peaceful species. Source: https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
67. Sleeping: at night it retreats into a hidden recess in the rock and forms a mucus cocoon. The discarded cocoon may be visible in the morning. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
68. Fishtankadvisor says they "spin mucus cocoons to sleep," which masks their scent. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
69. Jumping: wrasses are strong jumpers, so a tight-fitting cover is recommended, especially while a new fish settles in. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
70. Hiding: rockwork with cracks, caves and crevices, plus a tight-fitting lid (search snippet). Source: search results for "six line wrasse care" (snippet only; the Fishtank Advisor page was not confirmed for this point)

**Feeding and pest control**

71. Diet: amphipods, copepods, isopods, worms and tiny invertebrates. It accepts finely chopped meaty foods, flakes and small pellets. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia ; https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
72. Pest eating: bristle worms, flatworms, pyramid snails (Fishtankadvisor). Aquariadise adds urchins and nuisance snails. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1 ; https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
73. Reef Builders reports one comment saying a specimen "just love[d] flatworms." It does not mention pyramid snails or bristle worms. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
74. It sometimes cleans parasites from other fish (Reef Builders). Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia

**Tank, reef safety, lifespan, price**

75. Reef safety: Reef Builders calls it an "exceptional candidate" for reef tanks that "won't harm sessile invertebrates in any manner," with only very small motile invertebrates at risk. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2014/02/14/sixline-wrasse-pseudocheilinus-hexataenia
76. Fishtankadvisor lists it as reef-safe. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
77. It may eat small crabs, crustaceans and small snails (search snippet). Cleaner shrimp and coral banded shrimp risk was not confirmed. Open item.
78. Minimum tank: Fishtankadvisor says a single fish can live in 20-30 gal, but a school needs at least 100 gal. Aquariadise recommends at least 30 gal. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1 ; https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
79. Water: about 75-82 F, specific gravity 1.020-1.025 (search snippet). Source: search results for "six line wrasse care" (snippet only)
80. Lifespan: 5-10 years. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1
81. Price: $20-$40, with larger fish costing more (Fishtankadvisor). Aquariadise says about $30 for a nice specimen. Source: https://fishtankadvisor.com/six-line-wrasse-care/?amp=1 ; https://www.aquariadise.com/six-line-wrasse/
82. LiveAquaria and other US retailer prices were not reached in this batch. Open item.

**Compatibility notes (partial)**

83. Ocellaris clownfish: a hobbyist forum reports one six line attacked two new ocellaris clownfish and had to be removed (search snippet). Source: https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/fish-advice.416949 (snippet only)
84. Another forum reply says adding other wrasses or small fishes to a tank with an established six line may be a problem (search snippet). Source: https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/i-need-more-fish.214319 (snippet only)
85. Cleaner wrasse, blue hippo tang, pajama cardinal, engineer goby, falco hawkfish, lawnmower blenny, tailspot blenny, indigo dottyback, leopard wrasse, diamond goby, royal gramma, cleaner shrimp and coral banded shrimp were not confirmed in fetched sources. See the open items.

## 3. Yellow clown goby (Gobiodon okinawae)

**Size, body, colour, sex**

86. Size: about 3.5 cm (1.5 in). Danireef says it is often under 3 cm and the FishBase maximum is about 3.5 cm. Sources: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/ ; https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
87. Body shape: "compact body." Fin shapes were not found. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
88. Colour: "bright yellow," described as "intense yellow" (Aquahoy) and "brilliant, uniform yellow" (Danireef). It is solid yellow, with no pattern noted. Sources: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/ ; https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
89. Sex: bidirectional protogynous hermaphrodite. Fish start female and may change sex later (Danireef, Aquahoy). Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/ ; https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
90. No visible male versus female colour difference was found. Open item for the juvenile and sex-determination cues.
91. Reef Builders says the fish will "squabble, change sex, and form a pair." Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks

**Perching, territoriality, coral risk**

92. Gobies prefer hard branching corals such as Acropora, where they perch and find protection. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
93. Danireef describes resting as "sedentary." It says they shelter among branches. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
94. Aquahoy says the fish may become territorial with other gobies. It is otherwise peaceful and sometimes shy. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
95. Danireef first says the fish is territorial, then says it is "rarely aggressive or territorial." The two statements conflict. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
96. Aquahoy warns that perching can irritate corals. Problems occur if the fish stays on one colony too long. It recommends providing several structures. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
97. Danireef warns the fish can damage delicate coral tissue and kill small Acropora branches while clearing spawning space. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
98. Reef Builders says it "can nip at corals sometimes, but again it's not damaging." If it spawns on a live Acropora it may clear a small patch of polyps. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks
99. Danireef says the fish coexists peacefully with most corals, SPS or LPS, with caution around delicate Acropora. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
100. Aquahoy says it is "generally reef-safe but may cause issues with certain corals." Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
101. Reef Builders author calls it "almost 100% reef safe." Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks

**Diet and feeding**

102. Wild diet: microalgae, small invertebrates, zooplankton, and parasites found on corals. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/ ; https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
103. Captive diet: frozen Artemia, mysis and cyclops, plus live copepods and Artemia nauplii. Dry food should not be the main diet (Aquahoy). Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
104. Feeding frequency: Aquahoy says 2-3 times a day in small amounts. Danireef says once daily is usually enough with live rock, and 2-3 times without live substrate. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/ ; https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
105. The fish is a carnivore in captivity, doing best on small frozen meaty foods such as brine shrimp and mysis (search snippet). Source: search results for "yellow clown goby care" (snippet only)

**Breeding**

106. Eggs are laid on coral skeleton. Danireef: "attached directly to the coral skeleton," and the male usually guards them. The side of the coral is not specified. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
107. Aquahoy says the male seeks a flat surface, such as a rock or coral base. The female deposits eggs, the male fertilises and guards them, and fry hatch after 5-7 days. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/
108. Captive breeding (De Jong Aquaculture, Netherlands): parents laid 50-200 eggs on a dead Acropora skeleton, with about 90% hatching. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2025/01/29/de-jong-marinelife-successfully-breeds-gobiodon-okinawae-opening-the-way-for-eventual-commercialization
109. Larvae were pelagic for about 40 days, metamorphosing on 4 December 2024. Fish reached 1 cm about two months later and full development at 80 days. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2025/01/29/de-jong-marinelife-successfully-breeds-gobiodon-okinawae-opening-the-way-for-eventual-commercialization
110. Reef Builders says captive-bred supply is inconsistent. Proaquatix no longer produces the species, citing small egg clusters and low hobbyist demand. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2025/01/29/de-jong-marinelife-successfully-breeds-gobiodon-okinawae-opening-the-way-for-eventual-commercialization

**Tank, lifespan, price, popularity**

111. Tank: at least 40 L (10 gal) for a single fish, 80 L (20 gal) with other fish (Aquahoy). Danireef says at least 50 L, and a 25 L nano may work for a single fish. Source: https://aquahoy.com/yellow-clown-goby-gobiodon-okinawae-care-feeding/ ; https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
112. Reef Builders says it is suitable for tanks as small as 10 gal. It calls it the lowest-priced of the Gobiodon gobies. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks
113. Water: 72-78 F, specific gravity 1.020-1.025, pH 8.1-8.4 (search snippet from a retailer). Source: search results for "yellow clown goby care size reef safe" (snippet only)
114. Lifespan: not found in fetched sources. Open item.
115. Price: unverified. A pulserevops.com page lists $39.99 (Biota) and $24.99-$34.99 (LiveAquaria) for captive-bred fish. Another page gives $49.99-$59.99 for ORA. These pages contradict each other and are low quality (search snippet). Source: https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/aq0906 (snippet only)
116. Popularity: described as one of the best nano reef fish (Reef Builders article title). Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks
117. Predators: avoid large fish that prey on gobies. Its toxic slime does not deter all enemies (search snippet). Source: search results for "yellow clown goby care size reef safe" (snippet only)
118. Jumping: not found. Open item.

**Compatibility notes (partial)**

119. Keep it with small, non-aggressive tankmates, and avoid large predators (search snippet; the source is a retailer page, not fetched). Source: search results for "yellow clown goby care size reef safe" (snippet only)
120. Danireef says it is compatible with peaceful invertebrates and small, non-predatory fish. Source: https://www.danireef.com/2025/10/08/gobiodon-okinawae-the-little-jewel-of-the-reef-our-guide/
121. Gobies squabble with their own kind in small tanks (search snippet). Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2024/03/20/yellow-gobies-are-the-best-fish-for-nano-reef-tanks

## Compatibility summary (all three fish against the 17-species list)

The batch did not find a reliable source for most pairings. Only the following were found:

- Six line wrasse vs ocellaris clownfish: one forum report of attack (fact 83, snippet).
- Six line wrasse vs other wrasses and small fish: forum caution (fact 84, snippet).
- Six line wrasse vs smaller peaceful fish: bullying (facts 64-66).
- Clown goby vs larger predators: avoid (fact 119, 117).
- Mandarin vs pod-eating tankmates: a forum poster says a mandarin needs tankmates that do not hunt pods (search snippet; no URL confirmed). Food competition is also covered in fact 24 (https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/content/post/how-to-care-for-mandarin-dragonets).
- Pajama cardinal: shy, needs a group of at least three and hiding places (search snippet). Source: search results for "compatibility chart" (snippet only).

No source was found for blue hippo tang, falco hawkfish, indigo dottyback, leopard wrasse, cleaner shrimp, coral banded shrimp, engineer goby, lawnmower blenny, tailspot blenny, diamond goby, royal gramma or cleaner wrasse against these three fish.

## Design estimates used in the sim [L]

**Mandarin (`MandarinBrain`).**
- Pecking (facts 15, 22-23): 2-7 pecks per spot, 0.7-1.6 s apart. Hops of 2-7 cm along rock within 14 cm of the sand, ranging up to 45 cm from its night crevice.
- Food: each peck relieves 0.1 min of hunger at pod index 1 (`MicrofaunaModel.podPreyIndex(MANDARIN)`). That breaks even near index 0.5, so a mature tank feeds it and dry rock starves it (facts 24, 30-31).
- Captive-bred fish: 50% accept frozen food (facts 32-34), and only food near the bottom.
- Spawning (facts 39-43): a male-female pair spawns on about 35% of evenings, while the lights ramp down. He circles her with his dorsal raised for 60 s. They rise together for 30 s to 8 cm under the surface, release 30-150 eggs (an `EggsLaid` event; the pelagic eggs do not survive in the tank), then sink back.
- Pairing: two males fight (row mandarin_mandarin).

**Six line wrasse (`SixlineBrain`).**
- Hunting (facts 62-63, 71): darts of 4-15 cm at 1.8 × cruise speed, with 1-3 pecks per stop, fed like the leopard wrasse from `rockPreyIndex`.
- Bullying (facts 64-66, 83-84): after 2 days in the tank it chases and nips:
  - newcomers (for their first 7 days)
  - other wrasses
  - meek small fish within 25 cm of itself
  It leaves the dottyback, hawkfish, tangs and eels alone. Chases come every 10-40 min.
- Wrasse rivalry: pair row sixline_wrasses.
- Night (facts 67-68): sleeps in a rock hole, with the cocoon finished 10 min after it settles in.

**Yellow clown goby (`ClownGobyBrain`).**
- Perch (facts 92-93): in the crown of a tree-form coral (Kenya tree, leathers, organ pipe), at 0.55-1.1 × canopy radius up and 0.1-0.5 × to the side. It stays clear of rock and at least 6 cm under the surface. With no coral it uses rock tops. It moves to another spot on its colony every 1-7 min.
- Feeding: snaps plankton within 8 cm of its perch.
- Pairing (facts 89, 91, 121): two on one colony squabble every 1-5 min. After 4 bouts they pair, and the bigger fish becomes male. Pair row clown_goby_coral covers the squabbling.
- Predators: hawkfish do not prey on it (bitter mucus, fact 117).
