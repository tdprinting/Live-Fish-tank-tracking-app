# Aiptasia control: fact sheet for the reef sim

Research snapshot, 2026-10-20. Sources are web pages seen in search snippets unless marked "fetched".
Tags: **[src]** = sourced, **[anecdote]** = forum or hobby opinion, **[gap]** = not found, **[est.]** = my estimate.
Prices are retailer listings at time of search, often sold out, and in mixed currencies. Treat as rough.

## 1. Aiptasia (Exaiptasia diaphana / pallida)

**Appearance**
- Column: slender, brownish or whitish, translucent, up to ~2.5 cm long. Two rows of slits through which acontia protrude. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exaiptasia_diaphana]
- Oral disc: up to ~1 cm wide. Up to ~96 tentacles in a whorl, mostly fairly long, a few extra-long, a few short. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exaiptasia_diaphana]
- Colour comes from symbiotic zooxanthellae (Symbiodinium microadriaticum). Brown, grey or white depending on light exposure. [src: same]
- White-tipped tentacles: [gap] not found in sources.
- Typical hobby polyp size 0.5-5 cm: [gap] only the 2.5 cm column length above was found. The 0.5-5 cm figure is unverified.
- Note: Aiptasia diaphana (yellow aiptasia, glasrose, Med/E Atlantic) is a different species with up to 160 tentacles. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiptasia_diaphana]

**Behaviour**
- Full retraction into rock crevices when touched is common. One hobbyist reports retracting to "a little blob". [anecdote: https://archive.reefcentral.com/forums/archive/index.php/t-41665.html]
- Retraction speed in seconds: [gap]. A commenter says Aiptasia retracts "really quickly" compared with other anemones. [anecdote: same search cluster]
- Day/night expansion: sources disagree. Some animals extend only after lights out. Others are fully open under light. [anecdote]. Treat as species/tank dependent.
- Sweeper tentacles / acontia: acontia are ejected by contracting the column and extending the tentacles as self-defence. Exaiptasia rarely detaches and moves by pedal locomotion. [src: https://peerj.com/articles/2996]
- Stinging of neighbouring corals: sweep distance (cm), damage rate (%/day): [gap]. No study found on coral damage from Aiptasia sweepers.

**Pedal laceration**
- Pieces of pedal disc tear off, then grow tentacles and become new polyps. [src: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8757374/]
- Aposymbiotic lacerates grow tentacles earlier than symbiotic ones. Over 20 days, symbiotic ones end up with more tentacles. [src: same]
- Laceration rate (% per day or per week): [gap].

**Arrival in tanks**
- Introduced accidentally with live rock and coral frags. [src: Hydrobiologia-style paper, https://hmr.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1007/s10152-004-0210-6 ; check journal before citing]
- Can hide in sumps, overflows and rock crevices for a long time before being noticed. [anecdote: https://www.reef2reef.com/threads/aiptasia.1159365/]
- Percentage of tanks that get Aiptasia from rock or plugs: [gap]. No prevalence study found.

**When scraped or disturbed**
- Fragments regrow. Any tissue left behind can re-form a polyp. [src: laceration above; scraping is not recommended]
- Killing by injection can release spores or larvae. Dying polyps release spores, and injecting the base instead of the mouth causes spore release. [anecdote: https://reefbuilders.com/2007/03/27/aiptasia-anemone-removal-by-lethal-injection-kalkwasser-paste-with-syringe]
- Laser kills can release larvae. [anecdote: https://www.utahreefs.com/forum/erics-lasersreview_topic62210.html]

## 2. Aiptasia-X (Red Sea)

- **Application:** Kit has a 60 ml bottle plus syringe with straight and angled lock-on applicator tips. Inject near the oral disc so the anemone takes it in. Turn pumps off. [src: https://www.krakencorals.co.uk/red-sea-aiptasia-x-60ml.html , https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/aiptasia-x]
- **Visual effect:** Retailer claims the anemone dies within minutes. Polyp shrinks and is encapsulated in hardened globules. [src: retailer copy, same URLs. Not independently verified.]
- **Volume and coverage:** 60 ml bottle, rated ~100 anemones. A 500 ml refill is decanted into the 60 ml bottle. [src: retailer copy; same URLs]
- **Price (USD unless noted):**
  - 60 ml: $17.99 (LiveAquaria, sold out at time of search) [src: https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/aiptasia-x]
  - 60 ml (~2.02 oz): $29.49 (Aquarium Depot, backordered) [src: https://aquariumdepot.ca/products/red-sea-aiptasia-x-2-02oz]

- **Hobbyist view:** Thicker paste, nicer syringes, works well. [anecdote: https://www.reef2reef.com or Reef Central thread cited in the Joe's Juice search]

## 3. Joe's Juice (Blue Life)

- **Application:** Turn off powerheads. Shake. Fill supplied syringe. For aiptasia, place syringe over the mouth and feed a small amount. Anemone consumes it. [src: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/joes-juice-20ml.html , https://natureaquariums.com/products/joes-juice-aiptasia-eliminator-20ml]
- **Visual effect:** [gap] No visual description of death found. Retailer claims the anemone consumes it.
- **Volume and coverage:** 20 ml bottle. Coverage per bottle: [gap].
- **Price:** $9.99 USD, 20 ml (Bulk Reef Supply and Nature Aquariums). Aquarium Depot lists $19.99 sale price (backordered). UK Charterhouse £13.39 sale (sold out). [src: URLs above]
- **Chemistry:** Hobbyist forum says it behaves like a calcium hydroxide slurry and raises pH, alkalinity and calcium. Monitor. [anecdote: https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/joes-juice.153492]
- **Blue Life's other product (Aiptasia Rx):** 0.5 oz for $12.62. Use 1 cc per anemone, apply on top of aiptasia. Max 5 cc per 24 h. [src: https://www.saltwateraquarium.com/aiptasia-rx-0-5-oz-removes-aiptasia-majano-anemones-blue-life-usa/]
- **Efficacy:** Mixed. One hobbyist: "didn't work that great". Another: worked, anemones did not return. [anecdote: https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/aptasia-help.259316]

## 4. Kalkwasser paste (calcium hydroxide)

- **Recipe:** Mix kalkwasser powder or pickling lime (calcium hydroxide) with a little water into a thick paste. Target: near toothpaste texture, thick enough to draw up. [anecdote: https://reefbuilders.com/2007/03/27/aiptasia-anemone-removal-by-lethal-injection-kalkwasser-paste-with-syringe ; https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/kalk-help.715942]
- **Syringe:** Blunt-ended syringe, needleless. [anecdote: same]
- **Application:** Turn pumps off. Inject into the mouth, not the base. Remove the syringe. Leave for about an hour. [anecdote: Reef Builders 2007 article; utahreefs thread https://www.utahreefs.com/forum/aptasia-killing-cocktail-wanted_topic77481.html]
- **Dose:** Treat 3-4 polyps per day. Repeat next day on survivors. [anecdote: same]
- **Effect:** Dies. Calcium hydroxide is caustic. Gloves and eye protection. Keep off corals. Dripping kalk for tank chemistry does not clear Aiptasia. [anecdote: https://reefs.com/forum/general-discussion/264741-aptasia-control.html]
- **Price:** Pickling lime about $3 for a container at Walmart. [anecdote: https://www.utahreefs.com/forum/erics-lasersreview_topic62210.html]

## 5. Aiptasia laser tools

- **How it works:** Focused high-power beam. Anemone closes within seconds. Repeated exposure is needed. Any tissue left regenerates. [anecdote: https://utahreefs.com/FORUM/printer_friendly_posts.asp?TID=62210]
- **Price:** $200-400 typical for a powerful unit, some into the thousands. One forum buyer paid $100 for a 2 W unit. [src: AquaNerd https://aquanerd.com/?p=14664 ; forum https://forums.reefcentral.com/threads/aiptasia-laser.2010173]
- **Effect:** Positive but limited reviews. Some users say it takes several passes on big outbreaks. Others say it is good only for spot-treating isolated animals near corals. [anecdote]
- **Safety:** Eye damage risk from reflections. Yellow safety glasses do not protect. Glasses must match wavelength. [anecdote: utahreefs thread]

## 6. Berghia stephanieae (aeolid nudibranch)

- **Length:** Wikipedia gives "up to 20 mm" in one section and "mature individuals up to 5 cm" in another. Unreconciled. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghia_stephanieae, fetched]. Trade listings say 1/2-1 inch. [src: https://topshelfaquatics.com/products/berghia-nudibranch-aiptasia-destroyers-xl]
- **Colour:** [gap] Wikipedia has no colour description. Cerata have venomous cnidosacs at the tips. [src: fetched Wikipedia]
- **Crawl speed:** [gap]
- **Behaviour (night/day hiding):** [gap] Not found in sources.
- **Feeding:** Diet is strictly Aiptasia in the wild. Juveniles start preying on Aiptasia pieces about 48 h after metamorphosis. Feeding rate per adult per day: [gap] in the Wikipedia article. One hobbyist: one Aiptasia per day for a pair. [anecdote]
- **Egg coils:**
  - Egg masses are gelatinous, one capsule per embryo. First masses have 60-80 embryos. Mature masses have 1,000-2,000 embryos. [src: fetched Wikipedia]
  - Laying frequency: one spiral per day or every other day. [anecdote: breeding log in Reef Central search cluster]
  - Hatch time: 9-14 days (anecdote). Development is 60 days at 22 °C to maturity. [src: Wikipedia, fetched]
- **Lifespan:** 6-12 months with enough food. [src: https://www.aquariumsource.com/berghia-nudibranch]
- **Starvation after Aiptasia gone:** Will starve once Aiptasia is gone. [src: Wikipedia, fetched]
- **Predators in tank:** Some wrasses, carnivorous shrimp and filefish may eat adults or eggs. [src: retailer copy, https://topshelfaquatics.com/collections/all/products/berghia-nudibranch-aiptasia-destroyers]
- **Copper and parameter sensitivity:** [gap]
- **Price:**
  - 10-count pack: no current US or UK price found. [gap]
  - Old sale at $10 each (2017-era community sale). [anecdote]
  - Singapore: $15-25 per animal, cheaper in packs of 5 or 10. [src: guide snippet, https://gensou.sg/?p=34199]
- **Stocking:** XL Berghia: one per 30 gal, minimum 3. [src: Top Shelf XL listing] Supplier suggests 2-3 for light infestation in 10-30 gal, 4-6 for moderate in 40-75 gal. Larger groups work faster. [src: supplier snippet, likely https://topshelfaquatics.com/products/berghia-nudibranch-aiptasia-destroyers]
- **Flow:** Low to moderate flow, lots of rock. [src: supplier]

## 7. Peppermint shrimp (Lysmata boggessi vs wurdemanni)

- **Reliability:** L. boggessi described as the most dependable natural Aiptasia predator in the trade. Split from L. wurdemanni in 2006. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysmata_boggessi]
- **Research:** Rhyne et al. 2004 is the main basis. Secondary source: larger, mature shrimp eat more. Starved shrimp eat significantly more than fed ones. [secondary: search summaries; original paper not located; Hydrobiologia-style Rhyne 2004 may be doi:10.1007/s10152-004-0210-6, unverified]
- **Limitation:** Even true peppermints may not eat very large Aiptasia. [src: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/why-dont-my-peppermint-shrimp-eat-aiptasia]
- **Misidentification:** Common. Many sellers mislabel L. californica or other species as wurdemanni. [src: https://www.greenwave-aquatics.co.uk/pages/identification-of-true-peppermint-shrimp]
- **Nocturnal hunting, polyp size preference:** Nocturnal hunting: [anecdote]. Small-polyp preference: [gap]
- **Price:**
  - ORA captive-bred via Bulk Reef Supply: 3-pack $19.52, 6-pack $36.32, 12-pack $68.52. [src: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ora-peppermint-shrimp-qty-12-ora.html]
  - Tank-raised small L. boggessi: $10.50 (was $15.00). [src: https://www.saltwateraquarium.com/peppermint-shrimp-small-tank-raised-lysmata-boggessi-ora-chicago/]
  - GreenWave (UK): single £22.99, 2-pack £44.99 (sold out). [src: https://www.greenwave-aquatics.co.uk/products/peppermint-shrimp-lysmata-captive-bred-true-peppermint]

## 8. Aiptasia-eating filefish (Acreichthys tomentosus)

Reef safe with caution per every source. Reported to nip soft corals, zoas, SPS polyps, fleshy LPS, clam mantles and tiny ornamental shrimp. Individual behaviour varies, and Aiptasia control cannot be guaranteed. Minimum tank commonly listed at 30 gal. Price: LiveAquaria $38.99 sale (from $59.99, sold out) [src: https://www.liveaquaria.com/product/2562/?pcatid=256]; Bulk Reef Supply ORA captive-bred $59.99 and $74.99, out of stock [src: https://www.bulkreefsupply.com/ora-aiptasia-eating-filefish-ora.html]; Reef Exclusive $55 (sold out) [src: https://reefexclusive.com/products/aptesia-eating-filefish]. Plan for removal if it starts on corals. [src: https://reefs.com/fish/aiptasia-eating-filefish/]

## 9. Pest nudibranchs (hitchhikers)

**Zoanthid-eating aeolid nudibranchs.** Small aeolids that match the colour of the zoa they eat. They hide at polyp bases or under colonies. Signs: bite marks, closed polyps, egg ribbons. Eggs are small white spirals near zoas. [src: https://reefs.com/pest/zoanthid-eating-nudibranchs/ (403 on fetch, from search snippet)] Removal: quarantine the colony, dip and baste to dislodge adults, pick off egg masses, repeat dips every 4-7 days until none are seen. A fresh RO dip, then Coral RX dip, then tank-water dip removed most adults. Dips do not reliably kill eggs. Do not crush by hand. [anecdote: same source]

**Montipora-eating nudibranch (Phestilla subodiosus).** Formally described in 2020 as a corallivorous pest. Feeds on Montipora. Adults are easy to remove by brushing, dipping or waterpik. Eggs hide in convoluted Montipora crevices, so one pass rarely clears a colony. Life cycle poorly understood. [src: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7015969/ ; https://reefbuilders.com/2020/02/05/montipora-eating-nudibranch-finally-have-a-name-phestilla-subodiosus]
