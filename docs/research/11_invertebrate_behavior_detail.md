# Invertebrate behavior: feeding, movement, reproduction, and evidence gaps

Research update: 2026-10-12. This supplements [04](04_more_species.md) and the [fish update](10_fish_behavior_detail.md). Scope: the tank's mobile invertebrates and feather duster. Coral physiology remains in research 03/05/08/09. No simulation or app behavior is changed by this document.

## Evidence and identity first

**Direct study** means an observation or experiment on the stated species. **Analogy** means evidence from a different species. **Research proposal** means a suggested observation or model, not a measured result. Species-account and trade-name evidence is identified separately. Search-accessible abstracts support only what they actually report; an inaccessible full text is not treated as read.

## 1. Blue-leg hermit — Clibanarius tricolor

**Direct field/laboratory study.** Hazlett's Curaçao work describes daytime grouping and nighttime dispersal/feeding in *C. tricolor*, chemical orientation to groups and food, and shell fights. Smaller attackers rarely displaced larger defenders; recently molted animals were more vulnerable. Shell contests involve patterned interactions rather than merely two crabs touching. The wider study covered several hermit genera with differing daily and fighting patterns, so its results must not be applied indiscriminately to every hermit. [Hazlett, 1966, Naturalis repository](https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/506197).

**Research proposal.** Observe shell investigation, withdrawal, manipulation, contest, and actual transfer as distinct events. Record shell aperture, available empty shells, crab size, and recent molt evidence. Shell size alone is a poor observation record: a large shell with an unsuitable aperture may not be useful. A crab holding another shell is not proof that its occupant has been killed.

**Unresolved.** This source does not establish a tank-specific snail-killing probability, a fixed interval between shell changes, or a guaranteed peaceful outcome whenever spare shells exist. Before fitting a contest model, count encounters with occupied and unoccupied shells separately and document whether any transfer occurred. Do not deliberately create shell shortages to collect observations.

## 2. Scarlet hermit — Paguristes cadenati

**Species observation account.** The DORIS account describes feeding on animal and plant debris, greater nighttime activity, use of gastropod shells, separate sexes, and planktonic larvae. It is a curated natural-history account rather than a controlled trial quantifying aquarium grazing or aggression. [DORIS: P. cadenati](https://doris.ffessm.fr/Especes/Paguristes-cadenati-Bernard-l-ermite-rouge-de-recif-4381).

**Direct experimental context.** A trace-morphology study included *P. cadenati* among several arthropods and distinguished locomotion, resting, and feeding traces under different substrate conditions. It supports keeping substrate and behavior together when interpreting tracks; it does not establish a preferred aquarium sediment depth or a food consumption rate. [Devine & Minter, university research record](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/neoichnology-of-amphibious-arthropods-effects-of-subaqueous-and-s/).

**Research proposal.** Record where an individual feeds and how its shell moves with its body on sand versus rock. Track retraction as an interruption, not an instantaneous teleport into a different behavior. Keep its observation record separate from *C. tricolor*. No scarlet-specific shell-fight success percentage, molt timetable, or “one crab per volume” rule was established in this pass.

## 3. Sally Lightfoot crab — Percnon gibbesi

### Feeding evidence is more nuanced than “harmless” or “fish predator”

**Direct study.** A Sicily Strait investigation examined feeding structures and stomach contents of 19 specimens and concluded that the sampled crabs were herbivorous. This is useful evidence for grazing but not proof of an invariant diet in every habitat. [Puccio et al., 2006, author-shared paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225304425_Feeding_Habits_of_Percnon_gibbesi_H_Milne_Edwards_1853_in_the_Sicily_Strait).

**Direct study with geographic context.** A later southwestern Mediterranean investigation combined stomach contents and stable isotopes to examine seasonal diet. Its introduction explicitly contrasts earlier herbivory reports with reports of animal material. Stomach contents describe ingestion over a short interval; isotope evidence reflects assimilated resources over a longer interval. Neither method alone measures an aquarium fish-attack rate. [Seasonal trophic ecology study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0141113621002695).

**Direct feeding experiment.** A 2025 study tested grazing and preference using six macroalgae. This adds evidence that the identity of available algae matters, rather than every green surface being equivalent food. The retrieved abstract supports that experimental question and strong macrophyte grazing, not a universal per-day removal rate in a reef aquarium. [Pizarro-Borrull et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ece3.71686).

**Research proposal.** Separate picking algae, handling animal food, scavenging a dead organism, and attacking a live animal. For each event record food identity, size, shelter proximity, and whether prey was already dead. A hunger variable and body size alone are insufficient evidence for a fixed nightly fish-kill roll. A model can explore predation as a clearly labeled scenario, but these sources do not validate the current tank's probability.

## 4. Black brittle star — identity unresolved

### Locomotion

**Direct study, conditional on species.** *Ophiocoma echinata* uses coordinated arm movements to travel. Quantitative work distinguishes locomotor modes and shows that radial anatomy does not imply five independently random arms. Direction changes can be achieved by changing limb roles rather than rotating a permanent anatomical front like a fish. These results apply to the tested species; the owner's dark brittle star has not been identified from this research. [Astley, 2012, author-hosted paper](https://blogs.uakron.edu/astleylab/wp-content/uploads/sites/1471/2018/10/Astley-2012-Getting-around-when-youre-round.pdf).

### Feeding

**Related-species evidence.** Field observations of several *Ophiocoma* species distinguish deposit feeding and suspension feeding, with differences among species and settings. An arm held in a current can be part of feeding rather than locomotion. This paper is useful genus-level context, not a species identification or a basis for assigning the owner's animal an exact diet. [Habitat and feeding observations, Micronesica](https://micronesica.org/sites/default/files/habitat_and_feeding_observations_on_species_of_ophiocoma_micronesica_vol._19_no._1-2_dec._1983.pdf).

**Research proposal.** Observe the disc and each visible arm separately: disc sheltered with arms extended, food transport along an arm, coordinated travel, or withdrawal. Record disc diameter separately from total arm span. Do not turn any arm loss into guaranteed whole-animal reproduction or instant regeneration. Do not borrow predatory behavior from a different trade brittle star merely because both have five arms. Identification and repeated ordinary feeding observations are the next evidence needed.

## 5. Nassarius snail — Nassarius vibex when confirmed

**Direct study.** Lucena and colleagues investigated attraction to fish bait, feeding duration, and fasting effects. The reported field feeding bouts averaged about six minutes, with 76% lasting at most eight minutes; shell length did not explain feeding duration in that dataset. In laboratory trials, longer-fasted animals responded to food more quickly. This supports scavenging and a context-dependent response to food cues; it does not mean every animal must emerge after the same delay. [Lucena, Meirelles & Matthews-Cascon, 2012](https://periodicos.ufc.br/arquivosdecienciadomar/article/view/134).

**Explicit analogy.** An experiment on *N. festivus* found that hunger modified avoidance of damaged-conspecific cues. It is relevant to the question of risk-sensitive feeding but is not direct evidence for *N. vibex* or another trade Nassarius. [Hunger overcomes fear, 1995](https://academic.oup.com/mollus/article-abstract/61/1/55/998602).

**Research proposal.** Track the full sequence: resting/buried, cue response, travel, proboscis contact, feeding, departure, and reburying when observed. Record distance and flow direction relative to food. Do not count movement toward a food source as calories consumed. Do not assign the same algae-removal function as a grazing turban snail. The six-minute field result is a scale reference with variation, not a mandatory animation duration or feeding recommendation.

## 6. “Astrea” snail — working identity Lithopoma tectum

**Taxonomic record.** WoRMS lists *Lithopoma tectum* and distinguishes it from other *Lithopoma*. Trade usage of Astrea/Astraea and similar-looking shells is not enough to prove which species is present. [WoRMS genus records](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=taxlist&tName=Lithopoma).

**Related-species experiment.** A study of three aquarium cleanup gastropods found differences in grazing performance, survival, and growth among the tested species. Its tested taxa were not *L. tectum*. It therefore supports the need for species-specific evidence rather than borrowing its results as this snail's consumption rate or tolerance. [Gastropod cleanup-crew comparison, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6023205/).

**Research proposal.** Track attached crawling, feeding tracks, loss of attachment, foot contact during righting, and successful recovery separately. Record the substrate and shell orientation for a natural overturn event. “Cannot right itself” should not be an unconditional biological flag unless demonstrated for the identified animal and surface. This pass did not verify a *L. tectum*-specific righting-time distribution or a lethal timeout after inversion. Avoid simulated instant death merely because a snail is upside down in one frame.

## 7. Asterina-type sea star — asterinid/Aquilonastra candidate

**Direct movement study.** Time-lapse tracking of *Aquilonastra anomala* found movement consistent with a correlated random walk. Animals covered less distance with food present. That result provides a defensible alternative to choosing a new independent direction at every moment. It does not identify the owner's star or establish that every small asterinid eats coral. [Lohmann et al., 2016, institutional article record](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/sees_articles/270/).

**Comparative life-history analysis.** Asterinidae includes several reproductive modes: some species divide, while others have different egg and larval strategies. Fission is not a property to assign to every star sold as “Asterina.” The analysis explicitly distinguishes named fissiparous species from other family members. [Ebert, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1086/716913).

**Research proposal.** Photograph the same individual over time if recognizable, documenting arm number, asymmetry, size, and location. Separate apparent fission from injury or two different individuals. A star on a coral can be grazing surface material, contacting tissue, or simply crossing it; contact alone does not establish tissue consumption. This pass found no species-confirmed evidence supporting a universal organ-pipe attack rule for the owner's unidentified population. Record direct feeding evidence before using such a rule as a realism requirement.

## 8. Brown feather duster — Sabellastarte sp.

**Identity limitation.** Research 04 assumed a *S. indica*-type worm. Crown color alone does not resolve the species, so studies on *S. spectabilis* below remain analogies until identification improves.

**Species-specific research context.** A Hawaiian study describes larval development in *S. spectabilis*. Its existence supports distinguishing adult crown/tube behavior from a separate planktonic developmental phase. The accessible publication record does not justify inventing an adult crown-regrowth duration for the unidentified tank worm. [Bybee et al., larval-development paper](https://scientiamarina.revistas.csic.es/index.php/scientiamarina/article/view/132).

**Research proposal.** Record crown extension, flow direction at the crown, a rapid withdrawal, and gradual re-emergence as separate observations. Distinguish temporary retraction, loss of the crown, abandonment of the tube, and disappearance behind rock. Do not count every crown closure as feeding or death. A tube is not the whole animal, and its visible length is not a direct measure of body condition.

**Evidence gap.** This pass did not establish the particle-size capture spectrum, nutritional adequacy of a commercial liquid food, normal re-emergence delay, or a one-to-two-month regeneration clock for the owner's worm. To resolve those gaps, obtain identification and a species-specific feeding or regeneration study; do not transfer numbers from a different sabellid unnoticed. In ordinary observation, use naturally occurring food particles and changes in visibility rather than provoking repeated withdrawal.

## 9. What to record across invertebrates

The following is a proposed research record, not a set of claims that all animals behave alike.

| Observation | Required context | What it can help distinguish |
|---|---|---|
| Food contact | Animal identity, food type, capture/handling/swallowing evidence | Investigation versus feeding |
| Shrimp shell-like remains | Live animal later seen, shed-shell appearance, timing | Molt versus mortality |
| Hermit shell interaction | Occupant, sizes, available alternatives, outcome | Inspection versus displacement |
| Crab with another animal | Whether animal was alive before contact | Hunting versus scavenging |
| Brittle-star arm movement | Disc movement, substrate contact, visible food | Feeding versus locomotion |
| Snail inversion | Surface, orientation, foot reach, elapsed time | Recoverable overturn versus prolonged difficulty |
| Star on coral | Repeated position and directly observed tissue effect | Contact versus confirmed corallivory |
| Duster crown absent | Retraction, crown loss, tube condition, later emergence | Different events hidden by one visual state |

Record failures, withdrawals, and nonresponses. Use events per encounter or time in view; an animal hidden from the camera is not necessarily inactive. Body size measurements must say whether they describe carapace, body, shell, disc, or arm span. Keep individual state and population change separate: eggs or larvae do not imply successful recruitment.

## 10. Parameters not established by this research

Do not present the following as sourced constants: hourly cleanup capacity, universal molt intervals, starvation deadlines, fixed shrimp-to-shrimp kill chances, a crab's nightly fish-capture probability, inevitable snail death after inversion, or coral damage from every unidentified asterinid. Where a model needs a number before evidence exists, label it as a provisional estimate and state which observation would constrain it.
