**Access limits:** FishBase returned HTTP 403 or 503 on every fetch, so no FishBase fact below is verified. LiveAquaria returned 429 and Bulk Reef Supply was not reached. Facts marked (search snippet) come from search result text and were not confirmed on the full page.

## 1. Royal gramma (*Gramma loreto*)

### Size and body
1. Maximum size is about 8 cm (3.1 in). Wikipedia says the species "averag[es] slightly over 8 cm (3.1 in)" and the largest measured specimen was 8 cm. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
2. Reeflex gives 8-10 cm (3.15-3.94 in) and says males grow slightly larger than females. Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
3. Fishprofiles gives 8 cm for both sexes. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
4. Body shape (depth/length ratio) is not stated by any source fetched. Open item. Fishprofiles lists the tail as "Concave" and the mouth as "Normal" and nothing more. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto

### Color and pattern
5. The head is light purple to deep violet. The color fades mid-body to golden yellow at the tail. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
6. A small black spot sits on the front of the dorsal fin. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
7. A black line streaks through the eye. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
8. Eye color is not stated. The anal fin of both sexes has a minimal lilac stripe (keeper note). Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
9. Fin shape and fin color are not described in any fetched source. Wikipedia's only fin note is that the false gramma has clear fins, unlike the royal gramma. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
10. Juvenile vs adult coloration: no source fetched. Open item.
11. Male vs female: males are slightly larger. Sex is set during spawning by water temperature (gonochoric). Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm

### Swimming and habitat
12. Swimming mode: a mid-water fish that hovers near its retreat. Fin-use type (labriform vs body-caudal) is not stated in the fetched sources. Source for mid-water: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
13. Orientation: it tends to align parallel to the nearest surface, so it swims vertically or upside down beneath ledges. Wikipedia adds that this is normal and "not to be mistaken for illness." Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
14. Natural depth is 1-20 m (Wikipedia) or 1-60 m (Reeflex, FishBase-derived). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma ; https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
15. Range is the Caribbean and western Atlantic (Bermuda, Bahamas, Cuba, Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Belize, Costa Rica, Panama, Venezuela, Gulf of Mexico). Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
16. Middle-water dweller and diurnal (active by day). Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
17. Swimming speed and home range (in metres): not found.

### Behavior
18. Territoriality: it claims a territory in rock and crevices and is "very protective" of it. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
19. Threat display: when threatened it opens its mouth wide as a warning. It then retreats into a crevice quickly. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma ; https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
20. Inter-species aggression: it chases out small fish and can be aggressive toward tankmates in small nano reefs. It should not be kept with larger aggressive fish. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma
21. Fishprofiles warns against Pseudochromis and other basslets as tankmates. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
22. Dottyback conflict: a bicolor dottyback is said to attack a royal gramma. A 2002 Reef Central thread expects the gramma to dominate first, then the dottyback to take over once settled. These are hobbyist anecdotes, not controlled studies. Source: https://archive.reefcentral.com/forums/archive/index.php/t-64039.html?pda=1
23. Reefbuilders adds that the species defends large territories, so keeping it with similar-looking or shy fish is discouraged. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/06/19/the-royal-dottyback-pretty-but-potentially-a-royal-pain (search snippet)
24. Schooling: none. Groups of up to about 1,100 individuals are described in shallow ledge reefs in one guide (search snippet). That is unverified and conflicts with the territorial pair picture. Treat as an open item.
25. Cave use: abundant caves and overhangs are required. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto

### Feeding and day/night
26. Carnivore. Accepts brine shrimp, mysids, copepods, frozen foods and flake. Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm ; https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
27. Wild diet is reported as copepods and isopods (search snippet, source URL not retained, unverified).
28. Feeding frequency: not stated. Open item.
29. Sleep place and night color: not stated. Open item. Diurnal per fishprofiles, which implies retreat to a crevice at night.

### Breeding
30. Males build a nest of algae and plant debris among rocks. The female lays 20-100 eggs (Wikipedia) or up to about 400 eggs (Reeflex). Eggs are about 1 mm and hatch in 5-7 days, usually in the evening. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_gramma ; https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
31. Both parents are described as guarding the eggs (Reeflex). The male removes debris from the nest (Wikipedia). Sources as above.
32. Captive breeding has been done since the early 1960s (Reeflex).

### Lifespan
33. Not found in any fetched source. Open item.

### Care
34. Minimum tank: a 6 ft tank is suggested for a pair. Reeflex lists 35.2 gal (about 160 L). A keeper calls the stated 300 L minimum excessive and suggests about 150 L for a pair. Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
35. Temperature: 22-27 C (Fishprofiles) or 25.1-28 C (Reeflex). Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto ; https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
36. Salinity: specific gravity 1.022-1.026. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
37. Hardiness: "Average" difficulty. Shock after transport is common, and several buyers report deaths after shipping. Source: https://www.reeflex.net/tiere/437_Gramma_loreto.htm
38. Jumping: a known jumper. Use a hood. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
39. Reef safe: yes. Does not bother invertebrates and eats benthic crustaceans and worms on rock. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Percoids/Gramma_loreto
40. Compatibility with the sim list:
    - Dottyback (indigo, bicolor): risky. See facts 22-23.
    - Basslets and Pseudochromis: avoid. See fact 21.
    - Ocellaris clown, blue hippo tang, pajama cardinal, cleaner wrasse, engineer goby, falco hawkfish, lawnmower blenny, tailspot blenny, leopard wrasse, diamond goby, cleaner shrimp, coral banded shrimp: not researched in this batch.

### Price and popularity
41. Wild-type price: about $46-75 at Aquatic Unlimited (search snippet). Source: https://aquaticsunlimited.com/product/basslet-royal-gramma-gramma-loreto/
42. LiveAquaria listed one at $39.99 when sold out (search snippet). Source: https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/proaquatix-royal-gramma-basslet
43. Older US retail: about $10-20 (2016 Reef Builders) and an average of $22 in a cited study. Dated. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2016/07/25/captive-bred-royal-gramma (search snippet)
44. Captive-bred stock is sold by Biota (listing calls them captive bred). Source: https://shop.thebiotagroup.com/products/royal-gramm (search snippet)
45. Bulk Reef Supply price and captive-bred status: not reached. Open item.
46. Popularity: no species-level import ranking found in this batch. Rhyne et al. 2012 is the reference for US marine import data but its species table was not reached. Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70040343

## 2. Banggai cardinalfish (*Pterapogon kauderni*)

### Size and body
47. Maximum length is up to 8 cm (3 in) total length. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
48. Body shape: not described in the fetched source. Open item.

### Color and pattern
49. Three black vertical bars run across the head and body. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
50. The anal fin and second dorsal fin have prominent black anterior edges. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
51. Fins: the first dorsal fin is tasseled. The anal and second dorsal rays are elongated. The caudal fin is deeply forked. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
52. Eye color: not stated. Open item.
53. Juvenile vs adult: no source fetched. Open item.
54. Male vs female: the species is sexually monomorphic. Males are told apart only when brooding, because the mouth cavity becomes conspicuously enlarged. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish

### Swimming and behavior
55. Hovers above long-spined sea urchins (adults and juveniles) and retreats among the spines when threatened. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
56. Young fish are most commonly associated with sea anemones. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
57. Schooling: it hovers in loose groups near urchins. No explicit schooling data fetched. Open item for group size.
58. Swimming mode: hovering above urchins (fact 55). Fin-use type, speed, and home range are not described in the fetched sources. Open item.
59. Feeding: opportunistic. Copepods are the bulk of the diet. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
60. Feeding frequency: not stated. Open item.
61. Day/night behavior and sleep place: not stated. Open item.

### Breeding
62. The male mouthbroods eggs for up to 30 days and does not feed during that time. Eggs are 2-3 mm. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
63. Other sources give 20-30 days (search snippet). Source: https://gensou.sg/?p=32430 (search snippet, not verified)
64. Release size: not given. Young gain several times their hatching weight in the father's mouth. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
65. FishBase-derived note (search snippet): the young are released at about 8 mm SL, and newly hatched larvae have no planktonic phase. Source: https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Pterapogon-kauderni.html (FishBase blocked; snippet only)
66. Captive breeding is successful, and aquacultured fish are most of the market. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish

### Lifespan
67. About 4 years in optimal captivity; about 1-2 years in the wild. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish

### Care
68. Temperature, salinity, tank size, jumping, reef safety: not covered in the fetched sources. Open items. Use the reef-standard ranges from fact 35 as a placeholder only and mark it as an assumption.
69. Compatibility with sim list: not researched in this batch. Open item for all listed species.

### Conservation and trade
70. IUCN Red List: Endangered (version 3.1). The page cites a 2025 assessment, amended from a 2007 one, retrieved 18 August 2025. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
71. Wild trade started around 1995-1996. Exports reached 600,000-700,000 fish a year by 2001, and 700,000-900,000 a year for 2001-2004. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
72. The fish were still on the endangered list, and wild collection for the aquarium trade continues in the region (conservation note). Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/fishkeeping-news/captive-bred-banggais-from-tmc/ (search snippet)

### Price and popularity
73. Captive-bred price: as little as $14 US per fish as of early 2018. The figure is dated. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggai_cardinalfish
74. Captive-bred stock from an exclusive UK supplier (TMC) is sold year round. Source: https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/fishkeeping-news/captive-bred-banggais-from-tmc/ (search snippet)
75. Current US retail price (2025-26): not found. The Biota listing was a placeholder and is not reliable. Source: https://shop.thebiotagroup.com/products/banggai-cardinalfish (search snippet)
76. Popularity: a follow-up study on the same US invoice data as Rhyne et al. examined Banggai and orange clownfish trade. It reports roughly 9-11 million individual fish imported per year in the mid-2000s. It is unclear whether that figure is Banggai-specific. Source: https://marineinfo.org/doc/publication/288430 (search snippet)

## 3. Yellow watchman goby (*Cryptocentrus cinctus*) and pistol shrimp symbiosis

### Size and body
77. Maximum size: about 4 in (about 10 cm) per Reef Builders and about 7 cm (2.8 in) per Fishprofiles. Sources disagree. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby ; https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
78. Body shape: torpedo-shaped with two dorsal fins, a rounded tail, fused pelvic fins, high-set bulging eyes, and a large downturned mouth. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
79. Body length to depth ratio: not given. Open item.

### Color and pattern
80. Base color is yellow to golden brown, or whitish. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
81. White to light-blue dots cluster mainly on the front half of the body and speckle the dorsal fins. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
82. Fishprofiles lists "Spots Small" as its only marking. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
83. Eye color, fin color, juvenile vs adult color: not stated. Open items.
84. Male vs female: males show an elongated spine in the first dorsal fin. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus

### Swimming and behavior
85. Bottom-dwelling. Sand habitat (Fishprofiles lists "Sandy Marine"). Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
86. Western Pacific range. Depth commonly about 10 m (search snippet). Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus (search snippet)
87. Strong jumper, especially during acclimation. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
88. Temperament: peaceful and docile, but aggressive toward others of its own species. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
89. Grouping: when several gobies share a tank they usually split into isolated pairs, each sharing one burrow (hobbyist guide, search snippet, source URL not retained, unverified).
90. Burrow use: the goby lives in a burrow shared with the pistol shrimp. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby

### Pistol shrimp symbiosis
91. The partner is *Alpheus bellulus* (tiger pistol shrimp), which is how the pair occurs in nature. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
92. *Alpheus randalli* was not confirmed in any fetched source. Open item.
93. The shrimp has very poor eyesight. It continuously excavates the shared burrow. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
94. The goby acts as sentinel against predators. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
95. Antennal contact: the shrimp keeps at least one antenna touching the goby and senses danger from the goby's body language. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
96. Tail-flick warning: the goby flicks its tail rapidly to signal retreat (search snippet). Source: https://aquariumdepot.ca/products/yellow-watchman-goby-cryptocentrus-cinctus-biota-captive-bred (search snippet, not verified on full page)

### Feeding
97. Omnivore that prefers meaty food (brine shrimp, mysids). Microalgae is also a regular food. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
98. Feed at least twice daily with small meaty foods (mysids, brine shrimp, chopped clam, crustacean flesh, frozen carnivore blends). If tankmates eat first, target-feed with sinking pellets in the burrow. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
99. Day/night, sleep, and color change: not stated. Open item.

### Breeding
100. Pair spawning in the burrow: not confirmed in any fetched source. Open item.

### Lifespan
101. Not stated. Open item.

### Care
102. Minimum tank: 20 gal (Reef Builders). A retailer recommends at least 30 gal (search snippet). Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
103. Substrate: a mix of sand and rubble for burrowing. Cover the tank. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
104. Temperature, salinity: not stated for this species. Open item.
105. Reef safe: yes. "Won't pester or eat sessile invertebrates." Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
106. Whether it eats small shrimp: not stated. Open item.
107. Compatibility:
    - Shrimp gobies and conspecifics: avoid, unless a mated pair. Source: https://reefbuilders.com/2015/12/11/the-hardy-colorful-nano-friendly-yellow-watchman-goby
    - Large predators: exclude. Source: same.
    - Other small community fish: good, if not aggressive. Source: https://fishprofiles.com/profiles/marine/Blennoid_Gobioid/Cryptocentrus_cinctus
    - Dottyback, hawkfish, and the other listed species: not researched in this batch.

### Price and popularity
108. No current retail price was found for this species in this batch. Open item.
109. Captive-bred stock from Biota (search snippet). Source: https://aquariumdepot.ca/products/yellow-watchman-goby-cryptocentrus-cinctus-biota-captive-bred
110. Popularity: no bestseller or import ranking found. Open item.

## Design estimates used in the sim [L]

Values with no source above are the sim's own estimates, marked `[L]` in code and `Evidence.DESIGN_ESTIMATE`.

**Royal gramma (`GrammaBrain`).**
- Hangs by its cave: the reach grows over the first 5 days in the tank (new fish stay close), scaled by boldness.
- Belly-to-rock tilt (fact 13): starts within a clearance plus 1.5 to 5 cm of rock.
- Plankton picking: within 35 cm of the cave, or 12 cm in the first 2 days.
- Mouth-gape threat (fact 19): aimed at fish its size or smaller within 12 cm of the cave, followed by a short chase. Its mate is let past.
- Nest building (facts 30-31): a paired male fetches a strand of macroalgae or turf to the cave every 1-4 h.
- Sex: assigned at random. Two grammas form a pair or quarrel (pair row gramma_gramma). With a dottyback, the dottyback wins once settled (fact 22; row gramma_dotty).

**Banggai cardinal (`CardinalBrain`, Banggai branch).**
- Host: hovers over the nearest anemone (facts 55-56). Young fish under 3 cm sit 2.5 cm above it, adults 6 cm. With no anemone it hovers near cover, as the pajama does.
- Brooding: the male broods for 20-30 days (facts 62-63) and does not eat.
- Release: shows up to 6 young of 0.8 cm as real fish (fact 65, about 8 mm SL), with at most 12 Banggai in the tank. The rest are counted as rehomed. They never cross-breed with the pajama.
- Males are territorial toward each other (pair row banggai_males).

**Yellow watchman goby (`WatchmanGobyBrain`).**
- Burrow: in sand at the foot of rock, at least 20 cm from another goby's burrow.
- By day it lies just outside the mouth, head propped up about 14 degrees (facts 90-95).
- Alarm (fact 96): a 0.35 s tail flick, then it dives in and stays 15-60 s (scaled by skittishness). Its mate dives with it.
- Feeding: snaps food within 18 cm of the burrow and less than 12 cm above the sand (fact 98).
- Without a shrimp, it spits a mouthful of sand (0.25 cm³) out of the burrow every 20-60 min.
- Pairing: two watchmen added within an hour share a burrow half the time. Otherwise they chase each other (row watchman_watchman).
- Partner shrimp (facts 91-95): `alarmAt`, `burrow` and the "burrow" anchor are there for a pistol shrimp to read.
